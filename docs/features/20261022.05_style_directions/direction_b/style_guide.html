Manifest

Every readout line is a manifest line: what the company stated, set beside what Tricorder found, with the stamp it earned. A petrol-dark ground, an amber that works like a signal lamp for the one thing to do next, Caslon for the statements and Cousine for every figure.

01 Seed

How the seed shaped it

The datetime hash picked six things. Each one became a working rule, not decoration.

LinePickWhat it became
L.01Motif: shipping manifest and customsNumbered lines with a ruled gutter. Stated and found side by side, like declared and inspected. Box labels on panels. Verdicts as stamps. The words on screen stay the readout's own: stated, found, corroborated.
L.02Accent hue 27Signal-lamp amber, #F08B38 on dark and #A14E0C on light. It marks the next action and the found value, never a verdict.
L.03Libre Caslon Display, Libre Franklin, CousineCaslon for the big statements, like an engraved form header. Franklin for reading. Cousine for every figure, file name and line number.
L.04Airy, one idea per screenMarketing screens fill the viewport with one statement each. In the readout, one claim per line, with 24px of air inside every line.

L.06Pill controls on square panelsAnything you read is a square sheet. Anything you press or type in is a pill. Source chips are pills because they open something.

06 Rules

Apply without asking

  1. Set every figure, file name, cell reference and line number in Cousine with tabular-nums.

    Stated and found values are compared digit by digit, so the digits must line up.

  2. Put the stated value on the left and the found value on the right. Only the found side gets the 3px accent bar.

    The reader moves from what the company declared to what the check found, in that order, every time.

  3. Use the accent for the next action and the found value only: the primary button, focus, the ask line, the found label, index numbers.

    Amber sits near red. A verdict in amber would read as a warning it is not.

  4. Draw every mark as a stamp with its word and its shape. Never show a mark by colour alone.

    The partner may read a printout or be colour blind. The word and the shape still carry it.

  5. Set stamps straight. Never tilt them. Inside a sentence or a table, use the smaller .flat size.

    A tilt reads as playful, and this direction is serious.

  6. No short coloured bar over a heading or label. A top rule is allowed only when it lines up exactly with another vertical edge on the page and runs longer than the text under it.

    A bar that lines up with nothing looks like a mistake.

  7. Make anything you read a square panel. Make anything you press, type in or open a pill.

    One look at a corner tells the operator whether it does something.

  8. Number every readout line (L.01 for claims, P.01 for pieces) and give every panel a box label.

    Partners can say "line 2" in the meeting and everyone finds it.

  9. Use dashed lines only for what is absent or not real: gaps, not enough in the room, the synthetic label.

    If dashes mean one thing, a missing document is visible at a glance.

  10. Give each marketing screen one statement in Caslon and at most one supporting object. In the readout, one claim per line.

    The seed asked for air. Air also keeps a number from being read with its neighbour's source.

  11. Use Caslon Display at 1.5rem and above only. Never for figures or running text.

    Its hairlines break up at small sizes, worst on a dark ground.

  12. Show at most one accent fill on a screen.

    A signal lamp means one thing to do. Two lamps mean none.

  13. Put the synthetic label, word for word, on every page and panel that shows Hollis Voice figures.

    A screenshot travels without its context. The label has to travel with it.

  14. Use tokens only in components. Dark is the default; light is a mirror of the same tokens.

    A hex value in a component breaks the other theme without anyone noticing.
