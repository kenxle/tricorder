Direction C, style guide

Strip board

Tricorder lays a startup out the way a controller lays out a board of flight strips: one paper strip per piece of the company, each in its holder, each with its mark, its reading and its sources. The light ground is the board and the paper; the one dark band is the instrument, where the readout itself shows through.

06Rules a builder applies without asking

Rules

  1. One strip per piece, one piece per strip. The operator scans down a bay the way a controller scans a rack. Two findings on one strip hide one of them.
  2. A strip's fields are always in this order: tab, name, reading, action. The eye learns where the mark and the number are, and stops hunting.
  3. Only the top green and red flags are cocked out of the bay. A cocked strip means "watch this one". If everything is cocked, nothing is.
  4. Every mark carries its shape and its word, in the tab and in the name field. Colour alone fails for colour-blind readers, in print and on a projector at Betaworks.
  5. The accent never marks a finding. The olive belongs to the operator: buttons, links, focus, a steer. A finding in olive would read as the operator's own opinion.
  6. Green flag and strong are teal, not green. The accent's hue is 109; a true green beside it would read as the same family. Teal keeps "the documents agree" apart from "you did this".
  7. Every figure is Red Hat Mono with tabular figures, and right aligned in a table. Digits line up in columns, so two numbers compare at a glance.
  8. Every claim shows a source chip next to it, not in a footnote. "Every claim sourced" is the promise. A source below the fold is a claim without one.
  9. The dark band appears once per page, and the readout lives in it. One band keeps the instrument special. Two make the page a dark page.
  10. Panels are square; controls are pills. No radius in between, no shadow. The shape tells you what you can press.
  11. A steer is a note on the strip, never a rewrite in place. The scripts' numbers stay on screen beside the change, as the brand requires.
  12. A disagreement lists its sources side by side and marks no winner. The readout reports. The partner judges.
  13. A slot in brackets is never shipped as text. Counts and reasons come from the run. A bracket on screen means a scan has not filled it.
