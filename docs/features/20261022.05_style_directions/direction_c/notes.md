# Direction C: Strip board

- **Name.** Strip board. Each piece of the company is a paper strip in a holder, one row each, racked in bays.
- **From the motif.** Ruled strip fields with small mono box labels (Piece, Reading). A coloured tab at the strip's end that carries the mark's shape and a vertical word. The top green and red flags sit "cocked" out of the bay, the way a controller slides out a strip to watch it. A steer is the operator's pencil note riding on the strip. The band's edges carry a tick rule, like the rack rail.
- **Seed picks.** Hue 109 became a dark olive (light) and chartreuse (dark) used only for what the operator touches: buttons, links, focus, steer notes. The "looking strong" mark and green flag moved to teal (hue 190) so no finding ever wears the accent. Spectral names pieces, Work Sans carries sentences, Red Hat Mono holds figures, files and field labels. Pill controls and chips, square strips and tables. The one dark band holds the readout on both pages.
- **Risk taken.** A green flag that is drawn teal. It keeps the accent and the marks apart, but "green flag" in teal may read as odd to Ken. The fallback is to keep strong in teal and rename nothing, or to make the accent less green.
- **Also a risk.** Source chips sit inline in the reading sentence, not in parentheses. That keeps every claim beside its source, but long file names make strips tall on a phone.
- **Unsure.** Verdict lines for looking strong and mixed use bracket slots, because BRAND.md gives no Hollis Voice example for those marks; I did not invent figures. The homepage's steer note ("Lead with the runway on the full hiring plan.") is a made-up instruction to show the component, with the BRAND figures unchanged. In dark mode the band is only a little darker than the ground; the rails and tick rule carry the edge.
- **Fonts.** Google Fonts only, each with a fallback stack. The screenshots show the three faces loaded.
