Tricorder style directions

Three seeded directions, each with a style guide and a sample homepage. All three use the brand voice and the Hollis Voice figures from BRAND.md word for word. Pick one, or name what to take from each.

The seed

The script hashed the time the request arrived and cut the hash into three slices. Each slice picked a motif, an accent hue, a type pairing, a density, a ground and a corner shape. No two directions share a motif, typeface, density or ground.

Where they agree.

  • A wins the ten-second test: its first screen is a filled readout overview, every piece marked with its file. Both primary personas rated it strong.
  • B looks the most polished, but its first screen repeats the headline and shows no finding, so every persona ranked it last. Four of the five still asked to steal its big Stated / Found number pair.
  • C has the most trusted wording: "A green flag says the documents agree. It does not say the number is true." Its gap strip and steer note were also wanted on A. Its legend-first hero and "bay" metaphor cost it.
  • Four of the five want the data answer moved up and spelled out: where files are processed, whether a model provider sees them, how long anything is kept.
  • Three of the five (both primaries and the corporate VC) called "It reveals insight you can trust" the weakest line; the panels below it do the convincing. It is your line, so it stays unless you say otherwise.

A copy bug the reviewers caught. A and C say ARR is "stated four ways" and then show a three-row table; the fourth figure ($2,060,868, subscription rows) is missing. The same mismatch is in BRAND.md's own example, so it needs fixing there too.

Where primary and secondary split. The angel and the screener ranked C first because its strip board reads like a queue and handles thin rooms. The primary two outweigh them, and the screener's ask (a stack of applicants on one board) fits A's overview as well.
