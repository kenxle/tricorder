# Tricorder founder brief

Team Tricorder · Deal Flow NYC, Track 2: Diligence · Saturday 2026-10-24

**One startup, four ARR numbers:** about $3.0M on video, $2.5M in the deck, $2.24M in the model, $2.06M in the billing rows.

## Problem

VCs told the organisers a first pass takes an associate days of rebuilding numbers from a messy data room.

The red flag investors name most is numbers that disagree. A seed and Series A fund's guide: "When burn or revenue differs between the deck, the model and the accounting records, the gap suggests carelessness or something worse, and either reading damages the deal." ([seed fund guide, Aug 2026](https://www.crv.com/content/what-is-a-data-room))

The slow part is gathering and checking. On the pages we read, none of 24 tools shows market signals, comps and financial red flags together.

## Customer

The user is the associate at a seed or Series A fund who does the first pass by hand. The buyer is the partner or principal who signs for the fund's tools.

Funds already pay for this. A $600M fund uses a commercial tool that turns a data room into a memo ([vendor case study](https://www.v7labs.com/news/moderne-ventures-accelerates-due-diligence-with-v7-go)). One PitchBook seat costs $13,500 a year ([third-party roundup](https://www.failory.com/blog/pitchbook-pricing)). Both are second-hand; our interviews ask directly.

## Business model

We sell to the fund, per seat, yearly. We start from what funds pay for one data tool: Affinity lists $2,000 to $2,700 per user per year ([Affinity pricing](https://www.affinity.co/pricing)). It's a starting point, not a forecast. Our first pilot ends with a price.

Angels come next: they already pay for diligence by proxy, and memo tools sell at $12 a memo.

## The one workflow we'll demo

We open on the readout for Hollis Voice, an invented vet-clinic phone agent, run before we walk up.

1. **Green and red flags first:** the round adds up; the runway leaves out most hires.
2. **The overview:** every piece of the company, strong or weak, with its reason.
3. **Revenue, the weakest piece:** the four ARR numbers above, each opening its source. A pilot counts as ARR.
4. **The round, a strong piece:** the deck's $9M at $45M pre-money matches the cap table.
5. **Rooms we didn't plant:** scores on an outsider's sealed room, and one with nothing planted.

The operator can steer any piece.

**Next:** forward the pitch email, and the readout is already waiting.

**Where the data goes.** Tricorder reads only the documents you give it or the decks in mail you send it, keeps only the readout and its sources, and lets you see and delete everything it pulled. Today's data room is synthetic.

> Point it at a startup, get a readout: the best green flags, the worst red flags, and every number two clicks from its sources.
