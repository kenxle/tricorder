# Use of funds plan

*In short: the use of funds plan says what the round's money buys. Investors read it for milestones, not for a pie chart. The test is whether this much money, spent this way, gets the company to a point where the next round can be raised, and whether the runway the deck claims survives the hiring plan.*

Written 2026-10-22. Quotes were checked word for word against saved pages by [financials_quotes.py](raw/financials_quotes.py); results are in [quotes_financials.csv](quotes_financials.csv). Vendor and accounting-firm views are marked.

## 1. What it is

A short plan for the money in this round: how much, spent on what, over how many months, to reach which milestones. Most of the time it is one slide near the end of the pitch deck, called "the ask". Sometimes it is a spreadsheet budget. The CEO prepares it.

It is named in DocSend's checklist pages. Gale Wilkinson of Vitalize Venture Capital lists it among "other documents to consider":

> "a detailed plan for the use of the funds from this round" ([Gale Wilkinson, on DocSend](https://www.docsend.com/blog/4-types-of-documents-founders-must-have-in-their-fundraising-data-room-and-a-few-additional-tips/))

a16z asks for it inside the deck:

> "as well as a rough road map or plan for how you will use the funds." ([Justine Moore, a16z](https://a16z.com/the-insiders-guide-to-data-rooms-what-to-know-before-you-raise/))

## 2. At seed and at Series A

The 30-document table has no row for it. The checklist marks it as lighter at every stage, because DocSend names it only as an extra ([CHECKLIST.md](../../ongoing/CHECKLIST.md), "Use of funds plan for this round"). In practice it lives in the deck at every stage.

**What both stages share: raise for the next milestone.**

- YC, for seed rounds:

  > "Their goal should be to raise as much money as needed to get to their next “fundable” milestone, which will usually be 12 to 18 months later." ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/))

  That sentence is about companies that need a follow-on round. YC adds that the ideal is to raise enough to reach profitability.

- MaC Venture Capital, a seed fund:

  > "Typically, companies raise enough capital to fund operations for around 18 months." ([MaC Venture Capital, Kauffman Fellows journal](https://www.kauffmanfellows.org/journal/venture-diligence-at-the-seed-stage))

  > "It is important to know the company's gross and net current and ramped burn rates to ensure that the amount being raised will be enough to get them to the next fundraising round." ([MaC Venture Capital](https://www.kauffmanfellows.org/journal/venture-diligence-at-the-seed-stage))

- NFX:

  > "Requested investment should provide at least 18-24 months of runway ." ([NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/the-non-obvious-guide-to-fundraising))

- Haje Kamps, a TechCrunch writer who has founded companies and spent time in the VC world, says to think two rounds ahead:

  > "That means that in effect, a startup needs to think two funding rounds ahead when raising money: what you need to raise for this round, and the amount you will need for the next round, which will probably happen between 18 and 24 months from now." ([Haje Kamps, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/27/use-of-funds-are-not-percentages))

**What changes at Series A.** The raise is bigger, the hiring plan is longer and the next round is a Series B, so the milestones are revenue and efficiency numbers. The associate rebuilds the burn from the hiring plan, so the plan must match the cost lines. Visible (vendor) says seed and Series A rooms need that link:

> "Seed and Series A founders also need bios for the key team members, an org chart, and a hiring plan tied directly to the use of funds." ([Visible, vendor view](https://visible.vc/blog/what-to-include-in-your-data-room/))

**The sources disagree on the format.**

- Percentages or milestones? Kamps says percentages are the wrong form, and runway is the wrong target:

  > "You are not raising money to get 18 months of runway. You are raising money to hit a certain set of milestones." ([Haje Kamps, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/11/the-ask))

  8base, a software vendor, recommends a breakdown that may include percentages:

  > "This might include detailed percentages or figures for areas like product development, marketing, staff salaries or operational costs." ([8base, vendor view](https://8base.com/blog/use-of-funds-slide))

- How much buffer? The same author gave different answers a year apart. In 2022: "Add 30% to 70% as a safety buffer (depending on how good your planning is and how predictable your business is), and that's how much money you need to raise." ([Haje Kamps, TechCrunch, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/11/the-ask)) In 2023: "Then put a price tag on all of that and add a safety buffer of 20% to 40%, and that's the amount of money you need to raise." ([Haje Kamps, TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/27/use-of-funds-are-not-percentages)) Tricorder should not set a buffer threshold.
- Results or purchases? Wiss, a CPA firm, writes from the investor's seat:

  > "Reframe your points, focusing less on the day-to-day use of funds and more on the results." ([Wiss, vendor view](https://wiss.com/the-investors-perspective-how-pitch-decks-often-miss-the-point/))

## 3. What a first pass checks

1. Is there a specific dollar amount, and does it equal the raise in the deck, the cap table and the model?
2. Do the categories add up to the raise?
3. Is there a time frame (months) for the spend?
4. Are there milestones that can be measured and dated?
5. How many months of runway does the raise buy, and does that match the model and the deck?
6. Do the categories match the hiring plan and the cost lines in the P&L?
7. Is there a buffer, and does the plan survive a hire starting late?
8. Do the milestones look like what the next round needs?

## 4. Green flags

- **The ask is a number tied to milestones.** Kamps:

  > "Put differently: If I'm going to invest $2 million into your startup, I want to know what that money buys me, in terms of progress for your company." ([Haje Kamps, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/11/the-ask))

- **The goals are specific.** Kamps names the vague versions as the weak ones:

  > "Poor goals are vague: “Improve marketing,” or “Get more customers” or “Add features to our product.”" ([Haje Kamps, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/11/the-ask))

- **Wiggle room is built in.** Kamps:

  > "If you expect something to take 18 months, but it takes 24, it's a really poor look if you run out of money in month 19." ([Haje Kamps, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/11/the-ask))

- **The founder can say what each dollar buys.** Hustle Fund, an early-stage fund:

  > "There is no universal good burn rate. The useful test is whether the company can reach a valuable milestone, preserve options, and explain the evidence each dollar is buying." ([Hustle Fund](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-startup-burn-rate-management-what-investors-should-actually-watch-for))

- **Capital efficiency so far.** Jonathan Hua, who built diligence decks at two venture firms, says burn against progress decides trust:

  > "I look at burn against progress: a team that did a lot on a little earns real trust, and a team that burned a lot to produce very little earns uncompromising scrutiny." ([Jonathan Hua, The Data Room](https://thedataroom.substack.com/p/how-a-vc-does-due-diligence-on-your-company))

- **The hiring plan, the model and the raise agree.** Visible's line above; Kruze adds that hires start late, so a plan that already allows for it is a good sign:

  > "Build some slack into your model and assume most future hires start a month or two later than you would like." ([Kruze Consulting](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/build-a-rolling-cash-forecast/))

## 5. Red flags and pitfalls

**Common**

- **Percentages only, with no milestones.** Kamps gave a worked example and named the problem. On a split with 60 percent on operations:

  > "Spending 60% on operations makes me think that this is a very lopsided company." ([Haje Kamps, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/27/use-of-funds-are-not-percentages))

- **No slide.** Kamps: "Obviously, the easiest way to fail on this slide is to forget to include it altogether." ([Haje Kamps, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/11/the-ask))
- **A range instead of an amount.** Kamps: "Don't list a range." ([Haje Kamps, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/11/the-ask))
- **An amount that does not reach the next round.** From the 2023 column, written about a $5 million raise: "Are they sure $5 million is enough to get to where they are going?" ([Haje Kamps, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/27/use-of-funds-are-not-percentages))
- **Dodging burn and runway.** Hustle Fund:

  > "If they dodge questions about burn rate or runway, walk away." ([Hustle Fund, Angel Squad](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-how-to-verify-startup-claims))

- **A runway number that the model does not support.** NFX's 18 to 24 months is the usual floor (section 2); the 1.4 burn check rebuilds the real figure.

**Rare but serious**

- **Burn too high for the plan.** Jason Lemkin on a mistake he made as an investor:

  > "if the burn rate is too high, I find they tend to run out of money as they scale to $5m-$10m ARR." ([Jason Lemkin, SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/10-of-my-top-seed-investing-mistakes))

  And from a 2024 SaaStr post:

  > "Almost every founder regrets using capital to keep a high-burn rate engine going that isn't scaling rapidly." ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/every-top-founder-ever-i-should-have-acted-on-bad-trends-earlier/))

- **A raise that buys fewer months than the deck says.** This is the planted burn-and-runway flag in the demo room (section 9).
- **Uses that do not buy company progress.** I found no source in this set that lists repaying founder loans or a founder secondary sale as a use-of-funds red flag. Tricorder should still list every such line for the operator, with no comment on intent.

## 6. How Tricorder checks it

**What a script can check**

Existing:

- The "round adds up" corroboration: the raise amount in the deck, the model (`SERIES_A_RAISE`) and the cap table's new money agree (answer key, green flag "The round adds up").
- 1.4 Burn against the hiring plan and runway, `payroll_from_hiring_plan_and_rerun_cash`: rebuilds runway and compares it with the deck. It uses `runway_floor` for the 18-month floor.
- 1.5 Deck-against-model reconciliation, `deck_claim_vs_named_cell`, for the raise and the runway claim.

Proposed new checks:

- `use_of_funds_sum_equals_raise`. `SUM(category dollars) = raise` within a dollar. Show categories with dollars and percentages computed from the dollars.
- `use_of_funds_has_time_frame`. A stated period for the spend (months or dates). A gap when none is found.
- `use_of_funds_categories_map_to_pnl`. Each category maps to a P&L line group; list categories with no match.
- `use_of_funds_vs_hiring_plan`. For each department, the loaded cost of planned hires over the stated period against the category's dollars. Show both and the difference; do not flag without a period.
- `raise_buys_runway_months`. Rebuilt runway from entry 1.4, shown beside the deck's claim.

**What needs a model to read**

- Milestones: the measurable, dated goals in the slide and the plan. Quote each, or add a gap "Not in the room: milestones."
- Whether the slide gives a time frame.
- Whether the categories are the same ones the hiring plan and the P&L use.
- Any use that is not company spending (loan repayment, secondary). Quote the line and say nothing about why.

**What Tricorder cannot check**

- Whether the milestones are enough to raise the next round. That is a judgment for the operator and the partners; Kamps' test is to imagine what the company must look like at the next raise and price it.
- Whether the founder will spend the money this way. Ask for the monthly budget, and compare with later investor updates.

- **The pitch itself:** the checklist places "Use of funds plan for this round" and the check "Product roadmap against the use of funds" here (that check is not built yet).
- **Burn and runway:** the runway claim beside the rebuilt runway.

## 8. If it is missing

A gap at every stage, not a red flag. Because the plan usually sits in the deck, Tricorder looks there first and writes the gap only if the deck has no ask slide. Kamps treats a missing slide as the easiest way to fail; the readout only records that the item is not in the room.

One-line ask for the founder: "Please send how the round's money will be spent, by month and by category, with the milestones it reaches and the month you expect to raise next."

## 9. In the Hollis Voice room

The room holds it on deck slide 13, "The ask", with a related runway claim on slide 11 ([pitch_deck.pdf](../../../data_room/hollis_voice/01_overview/pitch_deck.pdf)). Numbers below come from [hollis_financials_facts_output.json](raw/hollis_financials_facts_output.json).

Slide 13 states the $9,000,000 Series A at $45,000,000 pre-money, 16.67 percent of the company, and this split:

| Category | Dollars | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering | $3,600,000 | 40 percent |
| Sales and marketing | $3,150,000 | 35 percent |
| Customer success and support | $1,350,000 | 15 percent |
| General and administrative | $900,000 | 10 percent |

- The four dollar amounts add to $9,000,000, and the shares computed from them match the slide.
- Green flag: the raise of $9,000,000 agrees across the model, the cap table and slide 13 (answer key, "The round adds up").
- The slide has percentages and dollars, no milestones and no time frame. By the sources above (Kamps) it is the weak form.
- "Customer success and support" is not a line in the model's P&L, where support specialists sit under sales and marketing. `use_of_funds_categories_map_to_pnl` would list it.
- The slide gives no period, so the planned hires cannot be compared with it. For reference, the 10 planned engineering hires cost $2,342,400 a year loaded at the model's 1.28 factor, against $3,600,000 on the slide.
- Slide 11 says "The plan funds marketing programs from the month the round closes." The model's Assumptions tab sets marketing programs at $140,000 a month from plan month 4, the month the round lands.
- Planted flag: slide 11 and the model say 37 months of runway, and the rebuilt figure with all 24 planned hires is 19 months (answer key; FRAMEWORKS 1.4). The use of funds therefore buys about half the runway the deck states.
