# Disclosure of claims, disputes and regulatory inquiries

Written 2026-10-22 for the Tricorder document guides. Quotes are checked word for word against saved pages (see `quotes_equity.csv`). Vendor and law-firm views are marked.

**In one line:** this is the company's own written list of lawsuits, threatened claims, disputes and regulator contacts, and most companies have something to list, so the signal is whether the list is complete and whether it agrees with the rest of the room and the outside world.

## 1. What it is

A written statement from the company that lists, or says there are none of:

- Pending or threatened lawsuits, demand letters and arbitration.
- Disputes with employees, former employees, contractors, customers or vendors.
- Contact from a regulator: a tax audit, a privacy or consumer complaint, a licensing letter, a subpoena.
- Consent decrees, injunctions, judgments and settlements.
- Anything similar involving the officers or directors.

The CEO prepares it, usually with counsel. At pre-seed it can be a paragraph in a cover note. At Series A it becomes part of the financing: the company makes a statement such as "the Company has no active litigation" in the purchase agreement, and anything that makes the statement untrue goes on a disclosure schedule. Cooley GO explains how that works: "If this statement is not true, then the company will need to disclose the active litigation on the disclosure schedule so that the representation and the disclosure schedule, taken together, constitute a true statement." ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/what-is-a-disclosure-schedule-and-why-do-i-need-to-prepare-one/)) The same article says the company writes the first draft, because outside counsel does not see day-to-day operations.

## 2. At seed and at Series A

The data-room research marks it expected at all three stages, with a star because the letters come from Cooley's request list (section D) and Hustle Fund's pre-seed baseline ([research_data_room.md](../20261022.02_research/research_data_room.md), row 30). Cooley GO's list asks for:

> "All documents and correspondence relating to any pending litigation, threatened litigation, or disputes which could potentially lead to litigation involving the Company, its executive officers or its directors." ([Cooley GO list](https://www.cooleygo.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Cooley-GO-Tip-Sheet-Sample-VC-Due-Diligence-Request-List.pdf))

Kruze's checklist, a vendor view, expects a list of ongoing or potential legal disputes (Maybe at pre-seed, Yes from seed on), an overview of regulatory compliance (Maybe through Series A, Yes at Series B and C), any pending or potential IP litigation (Maybe at pre-seed and seed, Yes from Series A) and a founder's own legal history (Maybe at the first three stages) ([Kruze](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/due-diligence-checklist/); [kruze_stage_rows.csv](raw/claims-disputes-inquiries/kruze_stage_rows.csv)).

- **At pre-seed and seed**, a short written statement. Hustle Fund's baseline: "Written disclosure of threatened claims, regulatory inquiries, or known disputes." ([Hustle Fund](https://hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-angel-investing-due-diligence-checklist-step-by-step))
- **At Series A**, the statement is backed by a formal disclosure schedule that the lead's counsel reads line by line. Cooley GO: "For subsequent financings, companies can generally start with the disclosure schedule from the prior round and update it". ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/what-is-a-disclosure-schedule-and-why-do-i-need-to-prepare-one/))

## 3. What a first pass checks

In order:

1. Is there a written statement, dated and signed by an officer, and does it list items or say none?
2. Does it cover threatened and potential claims, and not only filed suits?
3. For each item: who, what, the amount, the status, any insurance, and whether it touches IP, a key customer or an employee.
4. Employees, former employees and contractors: any claims, wage demands or severance disputes?
5. Regulators: tax, privacy, industry licenses, securities filings.
6. The founders' own history, including claims from a prior employer.
7. Settlements, consent decrees and judgments.
8. Does the statement agree with everything else in the room, and with court records and press?

## 4. Green flags

- **It is disclosed up front, with context.** REVERA, a law firm, to founders: "If you are involved in a dispute, don’t conceal it." The firm adds that disclosing, explaining and showing control "will give the investor confidence". ([REVERA](https://cyprus.revera.legal/?p=6158)) Kruze says the same of a founder's legal history: "Disclose if a founder has had any legal or regulatory issues - it is best to disclose this even if it is not asked for by investors." ([Kruze](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/due-diligence-checklist/))
- **A threat is listed even when it may come to nothing.** Aegis Law, a law firm: "The cost of disclosing something that does not materialize is essentially zero." ([Aegis Law](https://aegislaw.com/disclosure-schedules-ma-sell-side/))
- **A "none" that counsel stands behind.** Hustle Fund says a legitimate limit on what a company shares can be met with "A summary, redacted record, counsel confirmation, or staged access". ([Hustle Fund](https://hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-angel-investing-due-diligence-checklist-step-by-step))
- **The schedule is maintained.** The company started from the last round's schedule and updated it, which Cooley GO describes. A schedule that carries forward and changes by round shows someone is tracking.
- **The statement matches the outside world.** No court records or press that the statement leaves out.
- **Small matters are small.** REVERA: "Claims from former employees, disputes with contractors, or tax audits generally do not pose a problem for investors in and of themselves." The investor looks at consequences and whether it can be resolved. ([REVERA](https://cyprus.revera.legal/?p=6158))

## 5. Red flags and pitfalls

- **"None" when the room or the outside world says otherwise (rare but serious).** This is a difference in reporting between the statement and another source. Cooley GO names the cost of incomplete disclosure: "In financing transactions, failure to thoroughly disclose can result in all manner of negative consequences, including breach of contract damages or a retroactive (downward) adjustment to the investment purchase price (i.e., valuation)." ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/what-is-a-disclosure-schedule-and-why-do-i-need-to-prepare-one/))
- **A threat nobody wrote down (common).** Aegis Law: "In reality, most companies have something that does not quite fit a clean version of those statements." Its examples include a former employee who threatened a wage claim. An angry email naming a lawsuit "should be disclosed" in most cases. ([Aegis Law](https://aegislaw.com/disclosure-schedules-ma-sell-side/)) REVERA lists the quiet forms that investors' lawyers find: "a letter from a dissatisfied client, a pre-litigation claim, or a hint of dismissal “for cause”". ([REVERA](https://cyprus.revera.legal/?p=6158))
- **Disclosure in pieces (common to see, and reads as a behavior problem).** CRV, a fund, lists "Disclosure in pieces" among the room mistakes: one item surfaces, then a second appears only after an investor presses. CRV says it reads as a behavioral red flag. ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/what-is-a-data-room))
- **A founder and a former employer (less common, serious).** Goodwin partner William Wilson: "This exclusive ownership often faces risks when a founder moonlights on their new startup while still employed by another company, especially if the former employer offers a competing product or service." ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/22/10-ip-and-commercial-contract-loose-ends-to-tie-up-before-you-approach-investors)) Koley Jessen, a law firm: "These claims typically don’t manifest until the company has had some measure of success and the market has determined that its IP is valuable." ([Koley Jessen](https://www.koleyjessen.com/insights/publications/mother-knows-best-all-startups-need-to-have-a-clean-room)) So a quiet history does not prove a clean one.
- **An open co-founder dispute (serious).** Hustle Fund's pause list: "An unresolved co-founder equity, authority, or commitment dispute." ([Hustle Fund](https://hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-angel-investing-due-diligence-checklist-step-by-step))
- **A missed securities filing (rare but serious).** BCLP, a law firm, reports that in December 2024 the SEC settled actions against a private fund adviser and two pre-IPO companies for filing Form D late. The regulatory exposure is real even though it is easy to miss. ([BCLP](https://bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/sec-enforcement-actions-for-failure-to-file-form-d-a-wake-up-call-for-startups-and-private-fund-advisers.html))
- **Regulatory uncertainty priced into the deal.** Promise Legal (vendor) says investors who cannot judge a startup's data practices tend to respond by lowering the valuation or SAFE cap, slowing the close and asking for heavier disclosure schedules. It advises the company to "Prepare before the data room opens: data inventory + flows, security basics, vendor list, incident log, IP/data rights chain-of-title, and a remediation roadmap with dates." ([Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/startup-central/cap-tables-under-regulatory-pressure-how-ai-privacy-and-cybersecurity-risk-should-shape-fundraising-diligence-and-equity-terms-for-digital-health-startups/))

A note on how Tricorder words things. Hustle Fund: "Red flags concern truth, ownership, authority, or a risk that can kill the company." Its advice on a founder's background is to "treat a discrepancy as a prompt for context rather than an automatic finding of misconduct". Tricorder lists a mismatch, quotes both sides and leaves the judgment to the operator. It never says a company hid something. ([Hustle Fund](https://hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-angel-investing-due-diligence-checklist-step-by-step))

## 6. How Tricorder checks it

No FRAMEWORKS entry covers this document yet (the checklist marks it "not checked yet"). The checks below are proposals, except `present(type)`.

### What a script can check

- **`present(type)`** (exists in the pattern of [FRAMEWORKS 1.11](../../ongoing/FRAMEWORKS.md)). Is a disclosure statement in the file list? A statement that says "none" counts as present.
- **`claims_vs_room_text`** (proposed). Scan every text file in the room, including the workbooks, for a word list such as: lawsuit, litigation, dispute, demand letter, cease and desist, subpoena, inquiry, investigation, settlement, complaint, regulator, audit, notice of violation. Output one row per hit with the file, the line and the matched word. If the statement says none, or is missing, and any hit is real, show the hit beside the statement. The hits go to the model to read, because words like "claim" and "minutes" have other meanings in a room.
- **`legal_spend_spikes`** (proposed, report only). Formula: `ratio = payment ÷ median payment to that vendor`; list payments where `ratio ≥ k`, with `k` a setting. It asks "what was this for?" and does not conclude anything.
- **`court_docket_lookup`** (proposed, outside world). Search CourtListener's RECAP archive of federal court dockets for the company's legal name and each founder's name. The matches are for the operator to look at. Names collide, so a hit is never a finding. CourtListener's documentation gives default limits for authenticated users of 5 requests a minute, 50 an hour and 125 a day, so the lookup is rationed. ([CourtListener API](https://www.courtlistener.com/help/api/rest/)) The archive holds PACER dockets, and PACER is the federal courts' system, so state and county courts are separate searches.
- **`form_d_lookup`** (proposed, shared with the prior-financing guide). EDGAR Form D filing date against the first sale.

### What needs a model to read

- The statement: each item, its party, amount, status and type. The model must quote the line.
- The room's own papers for anything that bears on the list: customer contracts (termination, penalties), minutes, option records, the pilot agreement.
- The founders' bios against the product and the outside world, for the prior-employer question.
- The word-list hits from `claims_vs_room_text`, to separate real mentions from false ones.

### What Tricorder cannot check and leaves to the operator

- State and county court records, liens and judgments. These need a paid search or counsel.
- Whether a threatened claim is real or an empty threat. REVERA's advice is to distinguish "empty threats and real threats", and to consult a lawyer if needed. ([REVERA](https://cyprus.revera.legal/?p=6158))
- What regulators have said in private letters.
- Whether a former employee, customer or contractor has a grievance. A reference call.
- A founder's legal history that is not public.

- **Outside world**, for docket and filing lookups.

## 8. If it is missing

- **Pre-seed, seed and Series A:** a gap at every stage, since Hustle Fund's pre-seed baseline and Cooley's list both ask for it. The label is `cannot_check`. An empty folder is not evidence of a dispute. The line is cheap for a founder to supply, because "none" is a valid answer.
- It becomes a red flag only if the founder declines to give a statement, or if a docket or the room shows a matter the statement leaves out. That judgment is the operator's.

**Ask-list line:** "Please send a short written statement of any pending or threatened lawsuit, demand letter, dispute with an employee, contractor, customer or regulator, and any regulatory inquiry, settlement or order, or state that there are none."
