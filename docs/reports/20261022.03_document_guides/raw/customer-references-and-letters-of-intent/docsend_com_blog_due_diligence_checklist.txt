Title: The Startup Fundraising Due Diligence Checklist | DocSend. Andrea Silvers. Updated March 19, 2026. Originally published April 5, 2020.

Checklist section headings and items, as on the page:

Due diligence item: References. Customer and personal references.
Due diligence item: Corporate records and documents. Organizational documents (articles of incorporation, articles of organization, bylaws, operating agreement, etc.); Shareholder meeting minutes, board of director meeting minutes, etc.; Company organizational chart; Same documentation for any affiliate companies.
Due diligence item: Business plans and financials. Formal business plan; Forecasts: Sales, purchasing, margin, customer growth, marketing spend, etc.; Balance sheets (from founding to present); Income statements (from founding to present); Tax returns (from founding to present).
Due diligence item: Market research. Industry trends; Total addressable market (TAM); Customer acquisition cost; Retention rate; Price, cost, and margin analysis; Prospective customer research; Trial results; Competitive landscape; Focus groups, interviews, surveys, etc.; Other research.
Due diligence item: Intellectual Property. All existing and/or applied for patents, trademarks, copyrights, and domain names; If you haven't applied for protectable assets, an explanation (preferably a formal legal opinion) describing the decision not to file for IP protection; Any IP assignments that were made to you from another company or inventor and associated recordation documents.
Due diligence item: Shareholder information and agreements. List of owners and percentage ownership interest; All existing stock and options and their value at issuance; Any agreements related to stock, options, grants, and other issuances; Vesting schedules related to any issuances of stock or options; Agreements related to voting rights of shareholders; Any regulatory or legal documentation related to shares and shareholders.
Due diligence item: Material agreements. Standard terms of service or use between your business and customers; Any agreements or understandings between your company and others with obligations exceeding $25,000; Property leases (real estate and personal property); Loans, mortgages, liens, or encumbrances against your company; Loans provided by your company; Insurance policies; Licenses of any company intellectual property to third parties; Licenses of third-party intellectual property by your company; Any standard form used by the company in the ordinary course of business (NDAs, purchase agreements, terms of use, etc.); Any agreements outside the ordinary course of business that could have a material impact on the business; Consulting agreements; Agreements with officers and directors.
Due diligence item: Risks and potential litigation. Correspondence and documents related to any existing or threatened lawsuit, proceeding, or investigation; Correspondence and documents related to any existing or potential regulatory investigations or compliance concerns.
Due diligence item: Employee relations and benefits. List of company employees including title, salary, bonus potential, commissions, exempt or non-exempt status, and contact information; Standard offer letter and/or any employee contracts; Agreements between the company and any employee and/or director; List any documentation related to any employee benefit (401(k), insurance, medical, stock option, etc.); Severance plans; Company handbook and any other company policy.
Due diligence item: Equity grants. Equity already promised to early employees, customers or consultants.
Due diligence item: AI/ML and data governance (New for 2026). AI model documentation: Architecture, training methodologies, performance metrics, and validation processes; Training data provenance: Documentation of data sources, licensing, and rights to use training data; AI ethics & bias mitigation: Policies and testing procedures to identify and mitigate algorithmic bias; Data privacy compliance: Documentation of GDPR, CCPA, and other regional data protection compliance measures; Third-party AI services: Agreements with AI service providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, etc.) and associated terms; Responsible AI framework: Internal guidelines for ethical AI development and deployment; Model security: Safeguards against model theft, adversarial attacks, and prompt injection vulnerabilities; Regulatory preparedness: Documentation showing awareness of and preparation for emerging AI regulations.

Context on the page: the checklist is framed as what investors ask for after a term sheet is signed ("You hooked an investor and you signed a term sheet ... The only thing standing between you and the investor's money is the due diligence process.").
