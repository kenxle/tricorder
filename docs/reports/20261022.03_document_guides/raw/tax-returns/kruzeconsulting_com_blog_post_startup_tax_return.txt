 Filing your startup’s first tax return is a key milestone in your company’s financial journey, and it’s where strong startup accounting really starts to matter. Done well, it keeps you compliant, supports future fundraising, and helps you capture valuable tax savings like R&D credits .

 Below, we’ll walk through what founders of VC‑backed, Delaware C‑corps need to know about startup tax returns, how startup accounting fits into the process, and when to bring in a specialist like Kruze Consulting.

Visit our guide to Startup Tax Forms to find out more information about other IRS forms startups need.

 Why your first startup tax return matters

 Your first return does much more than satisfy the IRS. It lays the foundation for your financial and investor story.

- Investors and acquirers will request copies of all prior tax returns during diligence , and missing or incorrect filings can slow or even derail a funding round or acquisition.

- Clean, accurate returns must tie to your financial statements , which is why quality startup accounting is critical from day one.

- Even if you are pre‑revenue or loss‑making, your company is generally still required to file federal and state returns and can often use early‑stage losses to offset future taxable income.

 Example: A seed‑stage SaaS startup that has booked losses for several years but filed correct returns can carry forward those net operating losses (NOLs) to reduce taxes once it becomes profitable.

 What startup accounting covers at tax time

 Modern startup accounting isn’t just bookkeeping; it connects daily transactions to tax compliance and investor‑ready reporting.

- 
 Day‑to‑day accounting. Keeping accurate, GAAP‑aligned books, reconciliations, and financial statements to support the numbers reported on your return.

- 
 Tax return preparation. Completing federal, state, and local income tax returns tailored to VC‑backed Delaware C‑corps and their multi‑state footprints.

- 
 R&D tax credits and incentives. Identifying and documenting qualified research expenses so that eligible startups can reduce income or payroll taxes.

- 
 Tax nexus and state expansion. Reviewing where you have employees, revenue, or property so you understand which states you must file in .

- 
 Investor‑grade outputs. Reconciling returns with your financials and capitalization table , so you’re ready for board reviews, audits, and diligence.

 For venture‑funded founders, partnering with a firm that specializes in startup accounting can significantly reduce the risk of missed filings or credits.

 Key deadlines and requirements for VC‑backed startups

 Most venture‑funded startups are Delaware C‑corps, which come with a specific set of tax obligations.

- Federal income tax return ( Form 1120 ) is generally due April 15 for calendar‑year C‑corps, with the option to extend to October 15.

- Filing an extension gives you more time to submit the return, but you still must estimate and pay any federal tax by the original due date.

- States typically require their own income or franchise tax return , annual report, and, where applicable, payroll and sales tax filings.

- You must file even if your startup lost money, and some states impose minimum annual fees or franchise taxes regardless of profitability.

 Because obligations vary by state, a startup accounting team that understands multi‑state rules and nexus can help you avoid penalties and surprise notices.

 What drives the cost of a startup tax return?

 The cost of a startup’s tax return depends on a few core factors, which Kruze tracks in its online tax return calculator.

- 
 Fundraising stage. Seed, Series A, or later‑stage companies tend to have more complex capitalization tables and structures, increasing preparation time.

- 
 Industry and business model. Fintech and marketplace startups often have more regulatory and transaction complexity than a straightforward SaaS company.

- 
 Nexus and number of filings. More states with employees, sales, or offices mean more returns and more work for your tax team.

- 
 Volume and complexity of transactions. International entities, intercompany arrangements, or high volumes of transactions increase the time needed to prepare accurate returns.

 As a very rough benchmark from Kruze’s experience, a relatively straightforward Series A tech company may spend around $2,000 for its annual tax return.

 How startup accounting supports R&D credits and NOLs

 Smart startup accounting helps you turn losses and R&D spending into future tax advantages.

- 
 R&D tax credits. Many technology and life‑sciences startups qualify for federal and state research credits that can offset income or payroll taxes, even before profitability.

- 
 Payroll tax offsets. Early‑stage companies with no income tax liability may still be able to use R&D credits against payroll taxes, freeing up cash for growth.

- 
 Net operating losses (NOLs). Accurate accounting and tax filings allow you to carry forward NOLs to reduce taxable income in future profitable years, subject to IRS rules.

- 
 Documentation. A startup accounting team ensures that project‑level costs, time tracking, and technical documentation are captured to support credit claims.

 This is an area where “do‑it‑yourself” filing often leaves money on the table, because credits can be complex to calculate and substantiate.

 Can founders file the startup tax return themselves?

 Technically, yes, but it’s risky for most venture‑backed startups.

- The corporate tax code, multi‑state nexus rules, and startup‑specific incentives like R&D credits change frequently and require specialized expertise.

- Errors or omissions can lead to penalties or amended returns, and they create red flags when investors or acquirers review your financial history.

- Founders’ time is usually better spent on product and growth, while a startup accounting firm handles compliance and optimization.

 For VC‑backed Delaware C‑corps, most investors expect you to work with a CPA firm that focuses on startups rather than filing on your own.

 What information your tax CPA will need

 To prepare a complete, accurate startup tax return, your startup accounting partner will generally request a standard data package .

- 
 Company details. Legal name, employer identification number (EIN), formation documents, and current address.

- 
 Ownership and cap table. Shareholder information, including Social Security numbers where required, and an up‑to‑date capitalization table.

- 
 Historical filings. All prior federal and state tax returns and key state registrations.

- 
 Financial statements. Year‑end balance sheet and income statement, trial balance, and general ledger.

- 
 Payroll and benefits. Payroll reports, contractor payments, and details on any stock‑based compensation.

 A startup accounting firm that works with VC‑backed companies every day will often provide a detailed checklist to streamline this process.

 Getting started: your startup accounting next steps

 If you’re approaching your first filing season – or if you’ve already raised capital and want to upgrade from DIY bookkeeping – the best time to tighten up your startup accounting is now.

- Close your books monthly so your financials are always ready for tax and investor reporting.

- Assess your state footprint and identify where you may have tax nexus due to employees, sales, or property.

- Work with a startup accounting firm to evaluate R&D credit opportunities and NOLs before you file.
