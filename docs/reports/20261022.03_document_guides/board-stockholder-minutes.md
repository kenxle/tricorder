# Board and stockholder minutes

Written 2026-10-22 for the Tricorder document guides. Quotes are checked word for word against saved pages (see `quotes_equity.csv`). Vendor and law-firm views are marked.

**In one line:** minutes and consents are the proof that the board and the stockholders approved each stock issuance, option grant and financing, and the sources disagree on how closely a first pass reads them, so a missing set is a question for the partner before it is a flag.

## 1. What it is

The written record of the decisions the board and the stockholders make. It has two kinds of paper:

- **Minutes** of meetings, written by a secretary, approved by the board later and signed.
- **Written consents** that approve an action without a meeting.

Typical subjects: electing officers, approving stock issuances and option grants, adopting the equity plan, approving SAFEs, notes and priced rounds, amending the charter, adopting a 409A report, and signing big contracts. Company counsel or the CEO prepares them, and they are filed in a minute book. Cooley GO describes the purpose:

> "Over the life of your company, you will be asked on any number of occasions to provide evidence of board approval of certain actions" ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/maintain-corporate-minute-book/))

The note continues with seed rounds, credit lines and an IPO as the moments when someone asks.

## 2. At seed and at Series A

The data-room research marks it L, Y, Y for pre-seed, seed and Series A: lighter at pre-seed, included at seed and Series A ([research_data_room.md](../20261022.02_research/research_data_room.md), row 21). The sources do not agree on how much weight it carries.

- **Kruze's checklist**, a vendor view, asks for minutes and actions for the last three years: No at pre-seed and seed, Maybe at Series A, Yes at Series B and C. Its reason: "Demonstrate evidence of board decisions and approvals." ([Kruze](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/due-diligence-checklist/); [kruze_stage_rows.csv](raw/board-stockholder-minutes/kruze_stage_rows.csv))
- **Hustle Fund**, a fund, adds it at seed: "Board and stockholder consents or minutes relevant to financing, equity, debt, and major contracts." ([Hustle Fund](https://hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-angel-investing-due-diligence-checklist-step-by-step))
- **a16z**, a fund, lists minutes among documents that can wait: "Unless we have a specific question, we’re generally not poring over these meeting minutes". ([a16z](https://a16z.com/the-insiders-guide-to-data-rooms-what-to-know-before-you-raise/)) The a16z post adds that minutes tend to be heavily redacted and that it usually looks at board decks if they exist.
- **Cooley GO's request list**, a law firm's, opens with them: "All shareholder actions and meeting minutes." and "All board actions and meeting minutes." ([Cooley GO list](https://www.cooleygo.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Cooley-GO-Tip-Sheet-Sample-VC-Due-Diligence-Request-List.pdf))

The reconciliation: a fund's deal team may skip the discussion in minutes, and its counsel still reads the consents, because the consents prove each issuance was authorized. At seed the useful set is the approvals behind the SAFEs, the stock and the options. At Series A the lead's counsel wants the whole minute book. The research recommends treating a missing document as a question for the partner, not a flag by default.

## 3. What a first pass checks

In order:

1. Is there an approval for each stock issuance, SAFE or note, option grant batch and the equity plan?
2. Is each approval dated on or before the thing it approves?
3. Are consents signed by every director, and minutes signed by the secretary?
4. Do the amounts, holders and dates in the approvals match the cap table?
5. Was stockholder approval taken where needed (charter amendments, for example)?
6. Do the minutes say anything that disagrees with the pitch (cash, customers, hiring, disputes)?
7. Are there gaps: a year with no record, or an obvious action with no approval?
8. Do the dates and file details look as though the papers were written when they say they were?

## 4. Green flags

- **A minute book kept as the company goes.** Cooley GO shows a layout: certificate, bylaws, board actions (the organizational consent, signed minutes, and written consents), and stockholder actions, with an index. "All written consents approving actions between meetings, executed by all board members (noting that any written action outside of a “live” meeting must be an action by unanimous written consent)." ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/maintain-corporate-minute-book/))
- **A consent for every issuance, in the data room before anyone asks.** Cooley GO says to organize the data room into folders that match a diligence list, "separate folders for board and stockholder minutes, stock records, employment agreements, commercial agreements". It also says a tidy company is scrutinized less. ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/cant-afford-disorganized))
- **Counsel in the loop on corporate acts.** Foley & Lardner, a law firm: "Your attorneys can be a really great resource for record keeping, making all your lives easier when diligence begins." ([Foley & Lardner](https://foley.com/?p=54098))
- **Approvals that match the cap table line for line.** Promise Legal (vendor): "A cap table is compliant when the ownership and fully diluted math in Carta matches (1) the signed transaction documents and (2) the company’s underlying corporate authority." ([Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/startup-central/the-carta-cap-table-compliance-playbook-for-startups/))
- **Board approval of each option grant.** Holloway: "All option grants must be approved by the Board, and priced at the time of board approval." ([Holloway](https://www.holloway.com/g/angel-investing/sections/legal-due-diligence-for-angel-investments))

## 5. Red flags and pitfalls

- **Missing approvals for grants and issuances.** Cooley GO names the cost. If board minutes, "stock option grant approvals, employee proprietary information agreements" or similar papers are missing, "then the best case scenario is that your deal will be significantly slowed down", and in the worst case the deal is derailed. ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/cant-afford-disorganized))
- **Rebuilt records.** Cooley GO: "The alternative is disorganization and, potentially, increased expense in terms of legal fees if it becomes necessary to recreate a record of and ratify those past missing actions." ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/maintain-corporate-minute-book/)) Capbase (vendor) says "Startups that fail to maintain accurate records of board approvals and other legal agreements will often pay huge legal fees to fix these problems after the fact." ([Capbase](https://capbase.com/why-and-how-do-you-maintain-your-startups-corporate-minutes/))
- **Backdated fixes.** CRV, a fund, on how a room is read: "investors can spot a fix you backdated after their request landed". ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/what-is-a-data-room)) A consent dated years earlier but created last week is a difference worth showing.
- **Approvals by chat message.** Capbase notes "some startups interact with their board members via WhatsApp group chat and send out contracts for board members to sign electronically to approve financing arrangements and stock option awards", while the law still expects minutes. ([Capbase](https://capbase.com/why-and-how-do-you-maintain-your-startups-corporate-minutes/)) Electronic signature is fine; an approval that lives only in chat is not a record.
- **A written consent that is not unanimous.** Under Cooley GO's rule above, an action outside a meeting must be unanimous. A consent missing a director's signature does not do the job.
- **Several defects over several years (rare but serious).** A North Carolina Bar blog case study, built on a hypothetical startup, shows that a company with a skipped board election, an unauthorized issuance, a defective charter amendment and a defective director appointment must find and fix each one. "When you discover only one defective corporate act, determining the requirements under the statute for ratification (e.g., board approval, shareholder approval, and/or filing of articles of validation) is perhaps relatively straight-forward." It gets harder with several. ([NC Bar blog](https://www.ncbarblog.com/tips-for-using-new-statutory-ratification-procedure-to-cure-defects-in-authorizations-of-share-issuances-and-other-corporate-actions/))
- **A defect that cannot be ratified (rare but serious).** Richards, Layton & Finger, a Delaware firm, reports the Court of Chancery held that "Section 204 of the DGCL (“Section 204”) may not be used to ratify a “deliberately unauthorized corporate act”". A financing a founder had voted down could not be fixed that way. ([Richards, Layton & Finger](https://www.rlf.com/nguyen-v-view-inc-the-court-of-chancery-discusses-the-contours-of-ratification-of-defective-corporate-acts-under-section-204))
- **Stockholder approval missing where it is required.** A Rutan & Tucker lawyer writes that when stockholders would have needed to approve the original act, they must also approve its ratification, and that notice goes to all holders. ([Rutan & Tucker](https://www.rutan.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/rutan-8.14.17.pdf))
- **Minutes that disagree with the pitch.** Not a defect in the minutes. A difference in reporting between the minutes (cash, a lost customer, a dispute) and the deck.

## 6. How Tricorder checks it

No FRAMEWORKS entry covers this document yet (the checklist marks it "not checked yet"). The checks below are proposals.

### What a script can check

A model reads each approval into rows (date, type, signers, holder, amount). Then scripts compute.

- **`approval_coverage`** (proposed). Formula: `coverage = approved events ÷ all events`, where events are every stock issuance, SAFE or note, option grant batch and plan adoption on the cap table and the financing papers, and an event is approved when a consent or minute names the same holder and amount with a date on or before the event. List every event with no approval.
- **`approval_date_order`** (proposed). `approval_date ≤ issue_date`, per event.
- **`signature_completeness`** (proposed). `signers ÷ directors`, where directors come from the organizational consent. Under the unanimous rule it must be 1.
- **`minutes_cadence`** (proposed, report only). Meetings and consents by quarter, so gaps show.
- **`recorded_date_vs_file_date`** (proposed, report only). Compare the date written in the paper with the PDF's own creation date. A late file date can be innocent (a scan, a re-save), so the result is a difference to show and not a finding.
- **`text_scan_with_context`** (proposed). A plain search for the word "minutes" mislabels phone minutes and meeting minutes (see section 9). Search for "board", "consent", "resolved" and "secretary" instead.

### What needs a model to read

- Each action: date, who approved it, the numbers and holders it names, and whether it is a meeting or a consent. The model must quote the line.
- Statements in the minutes about cash, customers, hiring plans, disputes or deals, set against the deck and model.
- Stockholder approvals tied to charter changes.
- Redactions: the model notes where text is blacked out, since a16z says minutes tend to be heavily redacted.

### What Tricorder cannot check and leaves to the operator

- Whether the set is the full minute book or a selection. Only counsel or the company secretary can say.
- Whether a meeting happened that left no record.
- Whether a defect can be ratified, and how. A Delaware lawyer decides.
- What the board is like: how directors argue, who dissents. A reference call with a director.

## 7. Which readout pieces it feeds

- **Legal and cap table.**
- A minute that contradicts the deck also lowers the piece the claim belongs to (burn and runway, customers or the pitch itself). The scan raises it as a conflict.

Checklist items ([CHECKLIST.md](../../ongoing/CHECKLIST.md)): board and stockholder minutes (the document); incorporation papers and minutes match the cap table and approve each financing; every instrument and grant on the cap table has a document.

## 8. If it is missing

- **Pre-seed:** not expected in full. Nothing to flag.
- **Seed:** a gap only for the approvals behind the financing papers and option grants. The label is `watch`, with an ask. The full minute book is optional.
- **Series A:** a gap, and the approvals behind issuances become a firm ask. Counsel for the lead will want them. If an issuance has no approval at all, that is a red flag for the operator to take to counsel, because it can mean defective stock.

**Ask-list line:** "Please send the board and stockholder consents or minutes that approved each SAFE, note, stock issuance and option grant, the equity plan, and the 409A report."
