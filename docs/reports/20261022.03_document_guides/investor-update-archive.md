# Investor update archive

Guide for the data-room document in row 26 of the 30-document table in [the data-room research](../20261022.02_research/research_data_room.md). Written 2026-10-22 from online sources only. Every quote was checked word for word against the page text saved under [raw/investor-update-archive/](raw/investor-update-archive/). The check list is in [quotes_metrics.csv](quotes_metrics.csv).

## What it is

The emails or PDFs a founder has sent to existing investors, usually monthly, usually with the same sections each time: headline numbers, wins, problems, and a few requests for help. The founder (often the CEO) writes them as they go. That makes it the one document in the room that was written before the raise, month by month, without knowing a new investor would read it. It arrives as a folder of PDFs, a set of forwarded emails, or an export from an update tool.

Visible says that across thousands of updates sent through its platform, "the ten most-tracked metrics are Revenue, Cash Balance, Runway, Headcount, Gross Profit, Gross Margin, EBITDA, ARR/MRR, Net Income, and New Customers." ([Visible](https://visible.vc/blog/what-to-include-in-your-data-room/)) That is a vendor's count from its own users.

Because it was written at the time, it is a record to compare against. An investor reads it for trend, for how the founder talks about bad news, and for whether earlier numbers agree with today's. Pitchwise, a data-room vendor, describes the habit: "They compare decks against spreadsheets. Spreadsheets against past emails. Past emails against current answers." ([Pitchwise](https://www.pitchwise.se/blog/the-real-purpose-of-due-diligence-in-early-stage-investing))

## At seed and at Series A

- **Pre-seed:** not expected. The research table marks it "N". Only a company that already has outside investors can have one.
- **Seed:** lighter, and worth including. CRV, a venture fund, says "We recommend including past investor updates, because they show how the business moved month to month and that you keep backers informed." ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/what-is-a-data-room)) Visible, a vendor that sells update software, says "A seed-stage company with a clean update archive demonstrates communication discipline that no static document can match." ([Visible](https://visible.vc/blog/what-to-include-in-your-data-room/)) The same page says most founders forget to include them.
- **Series A:** expected. Visible: "Updates show momentum over time in a way no static deck can, and by Series A investors expect to see them." Malpani Ventures, a B2B seed fund, lists "Investors will like to see how you’ve progressed. Good to show you take investor communication seriously and diligently" beside "Last 12 months investor updates". ([Malpani Ventures](https://www.malpaniventures.com/blog/due-diligence-data-room))
- **Cadence the sources expect:** monthly. NFX, a venture firm, says "Monthly updates are the sweet spot, with perhaps slightly more frequent updates during a crisis, and off-cycle updates as needed when there are big changes." ([NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/investor-updates-tough-times)) NFX also reports that of seed founders in its March 2023 survey of 870 or more founders who had raised $1 million or more, 60 percent updated investors monthly, 21 percent weekly, 3 percent daily and 16 percent quarterly (same page).

## What a first pass checks

In order:

1. **How many updates are there, and are the months continuous?** Compare with the months since the first outside money.
2. **How recent is the newest one?**
3. **For each update, do the headline numbers equal the metrics export and the model for that month?**
4. **Do the labels and definitions stay the same?** Visible: "don’t share bookings one month and revenue the next."
5. **Do earlier plans happen?** A hire, a launch, a closed deal, a raise date.
6. **Is bad news in them, with runway?**
7. **Does the latest update agree with the deck?**
8. **Do customers and pilots named in the updates appear in the customer file, and do they stay?**

## Green flags

- **A steady rhythm that starts early in the month.** Jason Lemkin of SaaStr: "Few things inspire more investor confidence than a prompt update the first day or week of the month." ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/10-things-that-make-your-investors-lose-confidence))
- **The same metric definitions every month.** Visible: "It’s also important to keep the way you measure these metrics consistent from update to update so investors can spot trends over time, rather than having to reinterpret definitions each month." ([Visible](https://visible.vc/blog/how-to-write-the-perfect-investor-update/))
- **Numbers that match the data room.** Visible: "When the same numbers appear in both your data room dashboard and your monthly update, the two reinforce each other instead of competing for an investor's attention." ([Visible](https://visible.vc/blog/what-to-include-in-your-data-room/))
- **Lowlights as well as highlights, with runway and a target for next month.** NFX's template has "Lowlights: What challenges, problems or losses did you deal with?" and "Also include your target KPIs for the next update." ([NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/investor-updates-tough-times)) The earlier next-month targets let an investor check later updates against them.
- **Bad news early, with a plan.** Lemkin: "Don’t hide bad news. In fact, run to it. Share it, but share it with an action plan." ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/10-things-that-make-your-investors-lose-confidence))
- **Grounded tone.** Lemkin: "Be positive, but grounded in data and reality."
- **Specific asks.** Visible's account of advice from Aaron Harris, a Y Combinator partner, puts metrics first, targeted asks near the top and a short recap last ([Visible](https://visible.vc/blog/tips-from-yc-using-asks-metrics-and-a-recap-to-power-your-investor-updates/)). That is a vendor's retelling and not Harris's own page.

## Red flags and pitfalls

**Common**

- **Gaps in the run, or a run that stops when things get hard.** Lemkin: "Many founders share less when things get tougher, but that’s backward." ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/10-things-that-make-your-investors-lose-confidence)) NFX: "We will assume the worst if we don’t hear anything." ([NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/investor-updates-tough-times)) Visible: "Skipping updates, especially during tougher periods, can create uncertainty or signal that something is being hidden." ([Visible](https://visible.vc/blog/how-to-write-the-perfect-investor-update/)) Lemkin on X, quoted on his SaaStr page: "If you don't even get monthly updates anymore, well, you just write it off" ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/4-reasons-to-send-investor-updates-even-when-times-are-tough)).
- **Spin.** Lemkin: "Crazy optimistic investor reports spook investors." ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/10-things-that-make-your-investors-lose-confidence)) And the surprise that follows: "Nothing undermines confidence more than an out-of-the-blue email that growth plummeted."
- **Sitting on bad news.** NFX: "Sitting on it is the worst possible tactic." ([NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/investor-updates-tough-times))
- **Metrics that change meaning.** Visible, above: bookings one month and revenue the next. Pitchwise: "Inconsistencies introduce doubt." ([Pitchwise](https://www.pitchwise.se/blog/the-real-purpose-of-due-diligence-in-early-stage-investing)) The same page says investors read inconsistency as instability, not necessarily as deception.
- **Updates and data room that disagree.** The Visible line above on matching numbers, read the other way round.
- **A data room that is only refreshed when an investor asks.** Visible: "A data room that is updated only when an investor asks signals poor operating discipline." ([Visible](https://visible.vc/blog/what-to-include-in-your-data-room/)) The same holds for an archive that stops long before the raise.

**Rare but serious**

- **A number that appears in one update and vanishes without a word** (a customer, a pilot, a hire plan, a revenue line). This is my reading, built on the way investors compare past and present, and no source reports a case.
- **Updates that look written after the fact.** Only the original emails, with send dates, can show when they were written. Tricorder cannot see this from a PDF.
- **Existing investors who tell a different story.** Lemkin says new investors "will reach out to them for diligence checks on how the company is doing." ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/10-things-that-make-your-investors-lose-confidence)) So the archive is also what an existing investor will be asked to confirm.
- A reason to send them even when the news is bad: Lemkin writes "Here’s an uncomfortable truth: founders are really good at lying to themselves." ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/4-reasons-to-send-investor-updates-even-when-times-are-tough)) A company with no archive may also have no habit of writing the numbers down.

## How Tricorder checks it

**What a script can check**

No script exists yet. The checklist marks both related items "not checked yet" (see below). Proposed checks:

- `update_metrics_vs_export`. For each update, take the labeled numbers the model has extracted (MRR, ARR, customers, cash, burn, runway) and the update's month, and compare each with the metrics export and the model for that month. Flag a gap above `match_tolerance`. Each flag cites the update file and the export row.
- `update_cadence`. List the update dates and the gaps between them. Flag a gap over `update_gap_days`, a setting. The sources say monthly. The 45-day default is ours and the team can change it.
- `update_latest_vs_deck`. The latest update's headline numbers against the deck's claims, with the same tolerance.
- `update_send_dates`. When the room holds original emails, the `Date` header of each; when it holds PDFs, the file's creation date. Report updates whose file date is later than the month they describe.

**What needs a model to read, and what it must quote**

- The numbers and labels in each update, with the update's date. Quote the line.
- A change of definition or label between updates ("bookings" then "revenue", "customers" then "paying customers"). Quote both.
- Statements of plan ("we expect to close X by June", "we will hire Y") and what a later update says about them. Quote both.
- Whether bad news appears and whether runway is stated.
- Names of customers and pilots, for a later match with the customer file.

**What Tricorder cannot check, so the operator does**

- Whether the updates were really sent, and when. Ask the founder for the original emails, or ask an investor.
- What existing investors think. A call to one of them is a reference check.
- Whether anything was left out of an update. Tricorder reads only what it is given.

## Which readout pieces it feeds

- **Traction and revenue** (main). **Burn and runway** through the cash and runway lines. **The pitch itself**, through the latest update against the deck.
- Checklist items: C04 (the investor update archive itself), C12 (earlier investor updates agree with today's numbers). Both are marked "not checked yet". C29 (current monthly burn and the bank balance behind it) is often answered by the latest update.

## If it is missing

- **Pre-seed:** not a gap.
- **Seed:** a gap, lighter. Not a red flag. Visible says most seed founders forget to include them, so a missing archive is common.
- **Series A:** a gap. If the cap table shows outside investors from earlier years and no updates exist, that is a question to put to the founder, because these investors would have received something. It becomes a red flag only if the founder says updates were never sent while holding outside money, or declines to share them.
- **Ask-list line:** "Send the investor updates you have sent since your first outside money, as the original emails or PDFs, with send dates."
