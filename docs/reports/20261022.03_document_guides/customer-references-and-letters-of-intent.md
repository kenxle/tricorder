# Customer references and letters of intent

Written 2026-10-22 for the Tricorder document guides. Quotes are checked word for word against the saved pages by [verify_quotes_customers.py](raw/verify_quotes_customers.py); each is listed in [quotes_customers.csv](quotes_customers.csv). A vendor view is marked as one.

## Summary

- **This document is a list and some letters; the real evidence comes from the calls.** Tricorder cannot make the calls. It can check the list against the revenue file and prepare the questions.
- **Series A expects it; seed treats it as lighter.** Reference calls come late, often after the partner meeting, and they get more formal at Series A.
- **The biggest green flag is a list that includes people the founder did not need to pick,** including a customer who left. **The biggest red flag is a letter of intent counted as revenue.**
- **Nothing in the Hollis Voice room covers it.** There is no reference list and no letter of intent. The room's ask list will request one.

## 1. What it is

Two kinds of document sit under this name.

- **A reference list.** Names and contacts of people an investor may call: customers, and also management, current investors and advisors. DocSend's checklist lists 'References' as its first group and describes it only as 'Customer and personal references'. ([DocSend, due diligence checklist](https://www.docsend.com/blog/due-diligence-checklist/)) A seed investor's guest post on DocSend says the reference list names key members of the management team, one or two customers, one or two current investors (if any), advisors, and other people who know the founder well. ([Gale Wilkinson, Vitalize Venture Capital, on DocSend](https://www.docsend.com/blog/4-types-of-documents-founders-must-have-in-their-fundraising-data-room-and-a-few-additional-tips/))
- **Customer letters.** Reference letters (a customer's written endorsement) and letters of intent, or LOIs (a prospect says it plans to buy or run a pilot). “Startups most commonly use LOIs to secure non-binding commitments from prospective customers, often as proof of market validation to share with potential investors.” ([Jake Stein, Common Paper](https://commonpaper.com/blog/what-is-an-loi/))

The founder prepares both. They arrive as a short document or sheet and a few PDFs. The calls themselves are not a document; they are what the investor does with the list. Jonathan Hua, a VC, says the question in his head on every call is “what do people say when they think it can’t get back to the founder?” ([Jonathan Hua, The Data Room](https://thedataroom.substack.com/p/how-a-vc-does-due-diligence-on-your-company))

## 2. At seed and at Series A

- **Pre-seed:** not expected as a file. Visible and the data-room table mark it N. For a pre-revenue company, LOIs are the stand-in for traction. “Letters of intent (LOIs) with dollar figures attached, design partners testing weekly builds, pilot commitments and pre-orders all count.” ([CRV, on startup traction](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-traction))
- **Seed:** lighter (L) for customer references and LOIs. Visible's stage table marks 'Customer reference letters / LOIs' as not needed at pre-seed, lighter at seed, and expected at Series A and growth. ([Visible, an investor-update vendor](https://visible.vc/blog/what-to-include-in-your-data-room/)) The reference list as a whole is a different item: the seed investor's guest post on DocSend (section 1) treats it as a must-have at every stage. Only the customer part is lighter at seed. Calls are few and lean on people who can speak about the founders. First Round: “At First Round, we typically have anywhere from 5-15 diligence conversations” ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/heres-what-you-can-really-expect-when-pitching-your-seed-stage-startup-at-a-vc-partner-meeting/)) (some with customers, some with former colleagues). The same article makes the customer part conditional: “assuming there are even customers to speak with” ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/heres-what-you-can-really-expect-when-pitching-your-seed-stage-startup-at-a-vc-partner-meeting/)), and says there often are none.
- **Series A:** expected (Y). CRV says that at Series A “customer reference calls turn formal” ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/what-is-a-data-room)). Peony places customer and reference calls in weeks 2 to 6 of a Series A process, with the lead calling top customers to verify retention and value. ([Peony, a data room vendor](https://www.peony.ink/blog/series-a-data-room)) The count rises. “Late-stage firms also call more references (13 on average) than early-stage firms (8).” ([Gompers, Gornall, Kaplan and Strebulaev, NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22587/w22587.pdf)) That survey counts references of any kind, not only customers.
- **Timing.** Pascal Unger, a seed investor, writes that “most VCs (or at least potential leads) will ask to speak to your customers (both existing as well as potential) at some point during a diligence process” ([Pascal Unger, seed investor](https://pascalsnotes.substack.com/p/how-to-go-about-customer-reference)) “You should expect these to occur late in the process – sometimes post-term sheet.” ([Sarah Marion, BetaKit Ask an Investor](https://betakit.com/ask-an-investor-who-are-the-best-references-to-provide-to-vcs/)) Another seed investor: “Most funds I spoke with only do reference calls at the end of the process, as part of confirmatory diligence.” ([Form Ventures](https://formventures.substack.com/p/team-talks-ii-founder-reference-calls))
- **How many calls.** The sources disagree. Pascal Unger says “2-3 customer introductions should be enough per VC firm” ([Pascal Unger, seed investor](https://pascalsnotes.substack.com/p/how-to-go-about-customer-reference)). A VC Beast guide says “For a seed deal: 5-7 calls minimum.” ([VC Beast guide, no named author](https://vcbeast.com/guides/how-to-conduct-customer-reference-calls)) It also says “For a Series A or B: 10-15 calls.” ([VC Beast guide, no named author](https://vcbeast.com/guides/how-to-conduct-customer-reference-calls)) Tricorder sets no call count; it shows the operator the list and its gaps.

## 3. What a first pass checks

In order:

1. Is there a list, with name, title, company, how long they have been a customer and what they pay?
2. Does each name match a customer in the revenue file, and are they paying now? A pilot is not the same as a subscriber.
3. Does the list include the largest customers, the oldest, and at least one who stopped?
4. Is each contact someone who uses the product daily, or only a senior name?
5. For each letter: who signed, with what authority, on what date, promising what, for how much, on what conditions, and until when?
6. Does a letter of intent say whether it binds? Most are written not to.
7. Are LOIs and letters kept out of revenue, ARR and bookings?
8. Which customers should the operator call first, and what should each call test?

## 4. Green flags

- **LOIs with a named buyer, a use case and a dollar figure.** CRV: “Letters of intent (LOIs) with dollar figures attached, design partners testing weekly builds, pilot commitments and pre-orders all count.” ([CRV, on startup traction](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-traction)) It adds “A waitlist you built from one tweet shows little.” ([CRV, on startup traction](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-traction))
- **A churned customer on the list, offered without fuss.** “Tell them you need a mix: power users, occasional users, and at least 2 churned customers or prospects who didn't close.” ([VC Beast guide, no named author](https://vcbeast.com/guides/how-to-conduct-customer-reference-calls)) “A confident founder who believes in their product will give you churned customers without flinching.” ([VC Beast guide, no named author](https://vcbeast.com/guides/how-to-conduct-customer-reference-calls))
- **A reference from outside the founder's circle.** Jonathan Hua, a VC, says: “I always ask for at least one reference outside the people you’d naturally choose.” ([Jonathan Hua, The Data Room](https://thedataroom.substack.com/p/how-a-vc-does-due-diligence-on-your-company))
- **A reference who tells the same story as the pitch.** “A reference who describes you the same way you describe yourself.” ([Jonathan Hua, The Data Room](https://thedataroom.substack.com/p/how-a-vc-does-due-diligence-on-your-company))
- **The contact who owns the relationship.** “The person who owns the relationship is much more important than seniority or prestige.” ([Sarah Marion, BetaKit Ask an Investor](https://betakit.com/ask-an-investor-who-are-the-best-references-to-provide-to-vcs/))
- **A named case study with a number.** “One detailed case study with a named customer and a quantified outcome is worth more than ten logos on a slide.” ([Visible, an investor-update vendor](https://visible.vc/blog/what-to-include-in-your-data-room/))
- **A founder who names the weak spot first.** “Naming your own weakest number before I find it.” ([Jonathan Hua, The Data Room](https://thedataroom.substack.com/p/how-a-vc-does-due-diligence-on-your-company))
- **An LOI backed by paper.** “LOIs usually need to be backed by signed agreements to be compelling to investors” ([Gust](https://gust.com/blog/traction-is-validation-you-dont-control/)) A design partner agreement is stronger than an LOI. “The most notable difference from an LOI is that a Design Partner Agreement is binding.” ([Jake Stein, Common Paper](https://commonpaper.com/blog/what-is-an-loi/))

## 5. Red flags and pitfalls

Common:

- **A letter of intent counted as revenue.** Hustle Fund's checklist tells angels to keep the categories apart. “Separate contracted revenue, recognized revenue, pipeline, pilots, letters of intent, and verbal interest.” ([Hustle Fund, angel checklist](https://hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-angel-investing-due-diligence-checklist-step-by-step)) “The prospect has no legal obligation to move forward after the LOI, so investors don’t give it as much weight as a signed sales contract.” ([Jake Stein, Common Paper](https://commonpaper.com/blog/what-is-an-loi/))
- **Logos with no context.** “Logos without context are read as filler.” ([Visible, an investor-update vendor](https://visible.vc/blog/what-to-include-in-your-data-room/))
- **Only friendly references.** “only offering references who are direct beneficiaries of your success, an investor, a close friend, a subordinate” ([Jonathan Hua, The Data Room](https://thedataroom.substack.com/p/how-a-vc-does-due-diligence-on-your-company))
- **Lukewarm champions.** The BetaKit column (byline Sarah Marion) says of on-list references: “If they don’t love you or your product, that’s a red flag!” ([Sarah Marion, BetaKit Ask an Investor](https://betakit.com/ask-an-investor-who-are-the-best-references-to-provide-to-vcs/))
- **Scripted answers.** “References that sound like they’re reading from a script you gave them.” ([Jonathan Hua, The Data Room](https://thedataroom.substack.com/p/how-a-vc-does-due-diligence-on-your-company)) A VC Beast guide gives the same sign: “When you hear three references all use the same language (“game-changer,” “saves us X hours per week,” “the team is incredible”), that's a coaching signal.” ([VC Beast guide, no named author](https://vcbeast.com/guides/how-to-conduct-customer-reference-calls))
- **A founder who will not give a lost customer or a second name.** A VC Beast guide says a confident founder gives up churned customers easily, while a defensive founder pushes back, offers excuses, or conveniently has no contacts left, and the investor should note it. ([VC Beast guide, no named author](https://vcbeast.com/guides/how-to-conduct-customer-reference-calls)) On documents in general, Hua says “What’s still missing after multiple requests is rarely benign.” ([Jonathan Hua, The Data Room](https://thedataroom.substack.com/p/how-a-vc-does-due-diligence-on-your-company))
- **LOIs do not bind.** Hustle Fund tells founders to collect them, and still says “the offer is not technically binding” ([Hustle Fund, Apr 2026](https://hustlefund.vc/blog-posts-founders/how-do-we-best-position-our-seemingly-slow-growth-to-investors-in-order-to-raise-our-seed)) An investor reads an LOI as intent, not as a sale. Learning Loop explains why a signed letter beats talk: “customers may verbally endorse your idea while never intending to buy it” ([Learning Loop, a validation toolkit](https://learningloop.io/plays/letter-of-intent))
- **Overusing the customers.** Pascal Unger notes a signaling risk for early founders: customers do not want to work with a startup that may go out of business because its fundraise failed, so too many investor calls can hurt. ([Pascal Unger, seed investor](https://pascalsnotes.substack.com/p/how-to-go-about-customer-reference))

Rare but serious:

- **A reference that contradicts the deck.** “One customer reference that contradicts a retention claim can undo confidence late in diligence.” ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/what-is-a-data-room)) Hua: “I’ve had calls flip my recommendation mid-conversation” ([Jonathan Hua, The Data Room](https://thedataroom.substack.com/p/how-a-vc-does-due-diligence-on-your-company))
- **References who do not exist, never deployed, or turn out to be resellers.** A Gartner analyst reported that some vendor-supplied reference customers had not set the tool up in production, some turned out to be resellers, and some wins could not be verified to exist. ([The Register, 2013, second-hand report from a software buyer, not a VC](https://www.theregister.co.uk/2013/02/04/gartner_analyst_says_some_reference_customers_may_not_exist/)) This is a software buyer's account from 2013, not a VC's. It shows what a call can uncover that the list cannot.
- **A pattern of the same complaint across calls.** “VCs are more focused on patterns of negative feedback emerging, rather than one-off comments.” ([Sarah Marion, BetaKit Ask an Investor](https://betakit.com/ask-an-investor-who-are-the-best-references-to-provide-to-vcs/))

## 6. How Tricorder checks it

Entry 1.11 (What the room cannot settle) in [FRAMEWORKS.md](../../ongoing/FRAMEWORKS.md) already tests whether the room holds a customer reference list (`present("customer reference list")`) and writes an ask when it does not. The checklist marks the calls ("not checked yet") and the roadmap's reference-checks slice is low priority: Tricorder prepares the calls and never makes them ([ROADMAP.md](../../ongoing/ROADMAP.md)). Everything below the first item is proposed.

**What a script can check**

- Existing: `present("customer reference list")` in entry 1.11. Hollis returns false, so one ask is written.
- `reference_list_vs_customer_file` (proposed). Match each named customer to the customer file with the name rule entry 1.13 uses (lowercase, join "&" and "and", drop punctuation). Output for each name: paying now (`mrr > 0` in the as-of month), product type (Pilot or Subscription), first month, and share of counted MRR. An unmatched name writes the gap "Not in the room: a customer row for <name>", the same wording as 1.13.
- `reference_list_covers_largest` (proposed). A `watch` if none of the top three customers by MRR is on the list. The top three is the group entry 1.6 already uses.
- `reference_list_has_lapsed_customer` (proposed). An info claim says whether the list holds at least one customer who paid earlier and has `mrr = 0` now. This rests on one publisher's advice: “Tell them you need a mix: power users, occasional users, and at least 2 churned customers or prospects who didn't close.” ([VC Beast guide, no named author](https://vcbeast.com/guides/how-to-conduct-customer-reference-calls)) It is information, not a flag.
- `loi_counted_as_revenue` (proposed). A `flag` when a customer named in an LOI has counted MRR and no contract in the room. It is a narrower view of `counted_line_without_contract` in entry 1.13, run from the letter's side.
- `loi_expired` (proposed). A `watch` when a letter's own expiry date is before the as-of date and no contract matches the customer.

**What needs a model to read**

- Each letter: signer name and title, company, date, what is promised, any dollar amount, conditions, term, and the sentence that says whether it binds. Quote each, with the page.
- Whether a case study names a customer and a quantified outcome, with the sentence.
- A call sheet for the operator: for each customer on the list, questions drawn from that customer's data in the room (plan, start month, MRR trend, contract terms, pilot or subscription), each question citing the claim it comes from. The question topics from the sources: how they found the company, what they used before, how long going live took, what breaks if it is turned off, whether usage grew, whether they have looked at alternatives. The roadmap slice already limits Tricorder to preparing questions.

**What Tricorder cannot check (the operator does)**

- The call. Only a person gets the answer. Investors say the calls are often done poorly: “Most VCs do reference calls. From my conversations, few feel like they do them very well.” ([Form Ventures](https://formventures.substack.com/p/team-talks-ii-founder-reference-calls))
- Whether the person is who the list says, and whether they were coached.
- What customers who are not on the list say. Investors call these people too. Sarah Marion warns founders that investors also call people the founder did not list, such as portfolio companies that have been the company's customers or partners. ([Sarah Marion, BetaKit Ask an Investor](https://betakit.com/ask-an-investor-who-are-the-best-references-to-provide-to-vcs/))
- Whether an LOI will ever turn into a purchase.

## 7. Readout pieces and checklist items

Feeds **customers and contracts** (the main piece) and **traction and revenue** (LOIs kept out of revenue). It informs **retention** when a call asks why customers left.

Checklist items ([CHECKLIST.md](../../ongoing/CHECKLIST.md)): customer references and letters of intent, the document (C35); reference list covering management, customers, current investors and advisors (C97); letters of intent and verbal agreements kept out of revenue and bookings (C44); customer reference calls (C46). Founder reference calls (C58) are separate and belong to the team piece.

## 8. If it is missing

- **Seed:** a gap, not a red flag. First Round's partner-meeting guide says that at the seed stage there often are no customers to speak with. ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/heres-what-you-can-really-expect-when-pitching-your-seed-stage-startup-at-a-vc-partner-meeting/)) A pre-revenue company with no LOI and no pilot is simply "not enough in the room" for demand.
- **Series A:** a gap that the operator must close before the memo can call the customer piece strong. If the founder is asked more than once and still gives no names, treat it as a red flag. “What’s still missing after multiple requests is rarely benign.” ([Jonathan Hua, The Data Room](https://thedataroom.substack.com/p/how-a-vc-does-due-diligence-on-your-company))
- **Ask-list line (seed):** "Please send names and contacts for three customers we can call, including your largest and one who stopped using the product, plus any letters of intent."
- **Ask-list line (Series A):** "Please send a reference list with your five to eight most relevant customers, including your largest, your oldest and one who left, and any letters of intent with their dates and amounts." (The five to eight comes from the VC Beast guide; Pascal Unger would expect two or three.)
