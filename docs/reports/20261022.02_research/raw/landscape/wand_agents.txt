01 / Identity
A worker that persists.
A continuing entity holds its mandate, context and responsibilities across interactions. The models it uses can change while its place in the organization remains.02 / Initiative
Objectives create action.
A deadline, material signal or unresolved obligation can create the next task. The worker connects what changed with the outcome it is responsible for pursuing.03 / Judgment
Agency within authority.
The worker reasons through a situation and acts within its access, budget and policies. Decisions outside that boundary reach the designated human or agent authority.04 / Collaboration
Work crosses disciplines.
Agents coordinate with colleagues, request missing expertise and carry work between responsibilities. The organization can bring the right capabilities to a shared outcome.05 / Development
Experience changes the method.
Operating episodes can reveal patterns, inspire new strategies and develop reusable skills. Validated learning becomes useful when it reaches the next relevant decision.06 / Accountability
Results remain visible.
The worker’s actions, permissions and observed outcomes give management a basis for assessment. Performance means work completed and results verified.

Learning at the level of work
More than a longer memory.
Saving an episode is only the beginning. Its value comes from recognizing the pattern, learning what the outcome supports, and retrieving the relevant lesson at the moment of a later decision. The operating method can then change.

Return to work.
An unmet operating need can become a candidate improvement to a worker or a reason to develop a specialist. Evaluation establishes what the new capability can do, in which conditions, and with what evidence.
The organization gives that capability a role and appropriate authority, then observes its performance in operation. Development stays connected to the work that called for it.From individual to organization
