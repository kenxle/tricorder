A better relationship with technology.

How should humans interact with technology, and how could we make that interaction better?

It’s a question we keep coming back to.

We’ve spent decades building software to make our lives easier. Yet somehow, we’ve ended up spending much of our time managing it.

We switch between applications, chase updates, move information from one place to another, and learn to operate increasingly complicated systems that were supposed to help us.

We’ve become remarkably good at adapting ourselves to our tools.

We think it’s time our tools adapted to us.

Just because something works doesn’t mean it’s the best we can do.

We’ve never been particularly interested in accepting systems simply because they’re established.

Most conventions exist for a reason. They were solutions to real problems, built around the possibilities and limitations of their time.

But the fact that something made sense yesterday doesn’t make it the best solution tomorrow.

New technologies create opportunities to reconsider old assumptions.

We don’t believe everything needs reinventing. We just think some things genuinely deserve to be.

And right now, the way we interact with computers is one of them.

Technology should expand what people can do.

For a long time, the scale of what you could accomplish depended on the size of your organization.

More ambition meant more people. More projects meant more coordination. More opportunities came with more overhead.

We believe that’s beginning to change.

Imagine a world where a handful of extraordinary people can build what once required entire organizations.

Where independent businesses don’t have to choose between staying small and thinking big.

Where technology understands the context of your work, takes care of the things that need doing, and leaves you with more space to think, create, and make decisions.

We don’t think the future of work is about doing more work. It’s about making more things possible.

Having taste is easy. Making things with taste, consistently, not so much.

We’re fascinated by what happens when technology makes creation easier.

But when anyone can generate almost anything, the question of what deserves to exist becomes more important.

Taste matters. Judgment matters. Originality matters.

So does the craft of turning those things into something real.

We care about good engineering, thoughtful design, beautiful interactions, and products that feel almost obvious once they exist.

We reject unnecessary complexity, awkward experiences, superficial branding, and technology that looks impressive but makes people’s lives harder.

We want to build things that work beautifully.

And we want to become exceptionally good at doing that, over and over again.

Human agency is not negotiable.

As computers become more capable, the question of who remains in control becomes more important.

We believe technology should work on your behalf, without taking away your judgment.

That people should have meaningful control over their information, their tools, and the decisions that affect them.

That privacy isn’t merely a feature, but an important part of independence.

And that intelligence, however powerful, shouldn’t require us to surrender our autonomy.

We want a future where technology makes people more capable, not less human.

We want our work to represent something.

We’re ambitious about building yaven into a successful company.

But that’s not the whole ambition.

We’re interested in contributing something original. In exploring ideas worth exploring. In making things with care, curiosity, and conviction.

We’re more interested in helping shape the future of computing than simply building another software company.

That doesn’t mean we’ll always be right.

We’ll experiment, change our minds, discover things that don’t work, and probably spend an unreasonable amount of time debating details most people won’t notice.

But we believe the details matter.

We believe independent thinking matters.

And we believe building something genuinely different is worth the effort.

We’re starting with work.

More specifically, the unnecessary complexity that stands between people and what they’re trying to accomplish.

The endless coordination, administration, switching between tools, and keeping track of things that consume our attention.

We’re building yaven to take care of that work.

An AI teammate that understands the context of your business, helps move things forward, and gives small teams the capacity to pursue bigger ambitions.

We’re starting with independent businesses, agencies, and creative teams. People whose most valuable contribution isn’t managing software, but bringing ideas into the world.

It’s our first step toward a much bigger vision.

More independence. More possibility. More room to be human.

That’s the future we’re building toward.
