 PitchBook Pricing FAQ
 01 How much does PitchBook cost? 
 PitchBook does not publish pricing publicly. Based on 114 verified buyer transactions compiled by Vendr, contracts range from $20,000 to $124,000 per year, with a median of $30,000/year (Vendr data captured earlier in 2026; the Vendr page no longer loads, so these figures are unverified as of 2026-10-11). Uncited third-party estimates put single-user subscriptions at $12,000–$20,000/year. Team plans (3 users) commonly fall in the $18,000–$32,000/year range. All pricing requires a custom quote from PitchBook's sales team.

 02 Does PitchBook offer monthly billing? 
 No. PitchBook requires annual contracts — there is no monthly billing option. Multi-year commitments of 2–3 years are available and typically yield 15–30% discounts compared to one-year terms. Quarterly billing has occasionally been offered in individual negotiations.

 03 PitchBook vs Crunchbase: Which is better? 
 PitchBook and Crunchbase serve different needs and price points. Crunchbase Pro costs $49/month ($588/year) and focuses on startup and venture data with a self-service model. PitchBook is custom-priced (typically $12,000–$30,000+/year per seat) and offers institutional-grade private market data covering 6M+ companies, fund performance, LP profiles, and deep deal-level data. Choose Crunchbase for startup scouting on a budget; choose PitchBook for institutional-grade PE/VC intelligence.

 04 PitchBook vs Capital IQ: How do they compare? 
 PitchBook and Capital IQ (S&P Global) are both custom-quoted enterprise data platforms. PitchBook excels in private market data — VC/PE deal flow, fund performance, and investor profiles for 6M+ companies. Capital IQ is stronger for public company financials, credit ratings, and investment banking workflows with its 250+ Excel templates. Many firms subscribe to both. Both require a sales call for pricing.

 05 Is there a free trial of PitchBook? 
 PitchBook advertises a 'free trial' on its website, but multiple users report this is actually a guided sales demo rather than hands-on platform access. To test the platform independently, you must commit to a paid subscription. Students and faculty at participating universities can access PitchBook free through campus library subscriptions, with restrictions on data export volumes.

 06 How do you negotiate PitchBook pricing? 
 Key strategies: (1) Get competing quotes from Capital IQ, Preqin, and Crunchbase Enterprise before negotiating. (2) Commit to multi-year contracts for 15–30% savings. (3) Bundle multiple seat licenses for volume discounts — additional seats beyond a base plan are often quoted at ~$7,000/year per seat. (4) Time negotiations around PitchBook's fiscal year-end (December, aligned with Morningstar). (5) Push back on annual price escalations. Buyers who negotiate actively achieve average savings of ~14% below initial quotes, per Vendr data.

 07 How does PitchBook compare to Preqin and CB Insights on price? 
 PitchBook and CB Insights are generally on par with each other in pricing, though one PitchBook employee commented that data quality favors PitchBook. Preqin uses institutional bucket pricing aimed at larger organizations. For PE-focused use cases, community consensus tends to favor Preqin; for VC use cases, PitchBook is generally preferred. A competitor in the space launched at $12,000/year explicitly positioning against PitchBook's ~$31,000 price point.

 08 Can I negotiate the price of a PitchBook subscription? 
 Yes. Vendr data shows buyers save an average of 14% off list price. The primary documented lever is committing to a multi-year contract. Using competing quotes from Preqin, Crunchbase Pro, or newer entrants like Altss or Grata as leverage can also help drive down per-seat cost.

 09 Is PitchBook data accurate? 
 Data accuracy is a commonly cited complaint. Multiple Trustpilot reviewers report incorrect company information, outdated funding data, and profiles that misrepresent company status. One reviewer reported PitchBook listed their company as acquired by a competitor when it was not. That said, within the VC/PE professional community, PitchBook is still generally considered to have better data quality than CB Insights for deal and funding data.
