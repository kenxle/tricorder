 What We Fund, How We Decide, and How Long It Takes
 A founder’s guide to High Street Equity Partners, in about five minutes.

 Mitch 
 Nov 28, 2025 

 Share

 The most expensive thing in a seed raise isn’t dilution. It’s time. Specifically, it’s the weeks a founder spends in first meetings, follow-ups, and data room requests with investors who are out of alignment and thesis. 
 So here is ours, plainly. If you read this and don’t see yourself in it, that’s useful too. You just got a few weeks back.
 Where we invest 

 We back technology companies in three areas: Future of Work, Future of Care, and Emerging Technologies. We’re dual-headquartered in Washington, DC and Arkansas, and that pairing is deliberate. Geographically, we look hardest where capital is thinnest relative to talent: the Heartland and Midwest, the Mid-Atlantic, and the Southeast. We invest in founders who have historically been undercapitalized, not as a mandate but because we think the market has mispriced them. Being early to overlooked talent is what a seed fund is supposed to do. 
 The check 

 Fund I writes initial checks of up to $250K , and we hold 40% of the fund in reserve for follow-on checks of up to $250K more. We want to be one of your first five institutional investors. 
 We don’t lead rounds. We join syndicates. That’s a choice about where we’re most useful, not a hedge. A founder with a committed lead and a round to fill is exactly who we’re built for. A founder still looking for someone to set terms should know that before calling us.
 What we look for 

 We’re a post-revenue fund. Our sweet spot looks like this:
 An annual recurring revenue run rate of $300K or more 

 Less than $3M raised to date 

 A current round of $300K to $3M 

 A pre-money valuation or cap under $15M 

 Beyond the numbers: a U.S.-incorporated company with U.S. operations, a full-time founder, a real problem, and evidence that customers stay once they come. Retention tells us more than growth at this stage. Anyone can buy a first sale. Keeping the customer is the product working.
 What we pass on quickly 

 Some no’s are fast, and we’d rather you hear them now: web3 and crypto, cannabis, capital-intensive businesses, companies incorporated outside the U.S., and, for now, EdTech. We also won’t back a direct competitor to a company we’ve already funded. We owe our founders that.
 How it works 

 Application. Everyone starts with the same form, whether you came in warm or cold. It takes under ten minutes. If you’re not a fit, you don’t get silence. You get specific feedback on your deck and pointers to resources that might help. 
 Accelerated review. If you are a fit, we schedule a 90-minute call: five minutes of introductions, a ten-minute pitch, and the rest Q&A on your team, market, traction, capital needs, and where we can actually help. Ten minutes is not a typo. We want the story you’d tell a customer, not a 40-slide deck. 
 Diligence. Companies that clear review move into three focused sessions: a product and technology deep dive with your technical lead, an operational review with whoever runs operations, and a financial review led by our Principal Investor, Dr. Will Bowser. In parallel, we talk to industry experts and write an investment memo that usually runs 20 to 30 pages. 
 Investment committee. Our IC meets monthly. Sometimes we invite founders to answer questions we’ve prepared in advance. Either way, you hear a decision, from me, right after. 
 Close. Once agreements are signed, we wire within 48 hours. 
 Why we say yes, and why we don’t 

 We say yes when you fit the thesis, the problem is real, we understand exactly what this raise gets you to, the valuation works for both sides, and we believe in you as the person running the company.
 We say no for the opposite reasons, and sometimes for reasons that have nothing to do with you. We might have a similar company in the portfolio. We might be more excited about another deal that month. A small fund has to choose.
 A no from us isn’t the end of the relationship. Founders we pass on are invited to a quarterly 30-minute check-in with our general partners, and we’ll refer you to firms in our network that might fit better. A surprising number of our best relationships began with a no.
 The one piece of advice 

 Come early.
 The best time to meet us is before your round opens, at pre-seed, when there’s no clock running. We get to know the business without pressure, and by the time you raise your seed, we’re not starting from scratch. We’re deciding.
 The worst time to meet an investor is when you need one. Give us a reason to know you before then.
