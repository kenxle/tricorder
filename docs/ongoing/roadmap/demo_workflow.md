# The one workflow we demo

Back to the [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md). The current demo plan, with the beat sheet and fallbacks, is [DEMO.md](../DEMO.md). This page keeps the roadmap's earlier write-up of the demo's shape.

**Recommended: open on the top green and red flags, already waiting, then the overview, then drill into the weakest piece and one strong piece.** The first screen is the best green flags and the worst red flags for Hollis Voice's full room. Under them is the overview: each piece of the company with its mark (looking strong, looking weak, mixed, or not enough in the room) and its reason in one line ([writers' brief, 12:55 PM and 1:09 PM blocks](../../features/20261022.01_hackathon_plan/00_brief_for_writers.md)). The first click opens the weakest piece, traction and revenue. There the ARR figures sit side by side: about $3.0M said on video, $2.5M in the deck, $2.24M in the model, $2.06M in the billing rows, each with its source chip ([critique_demo.md](../../features/20261022.01_hackathon_plan/critique_demo.md), verdict; the exact figures are in `data_room/answer_key.json`, `facts`). The next opens one strong piece, the pitch itself, which is also the green flag: the round on slide 13, corroborated by the cap table, each source opening the other. This is true on stage: the run finished before the captain walked up. The body follows the [demo-script](demo-script.md) beat sheet: the green and red flags, the overview, the video sentence, the pilot clause, the strong piece, the gaps. Then one line reads out the runs on the sealed variant room and the clean room ([blind-room](blind-room.md)). The forwarded pitch email is the closing "next" line: forward it, and the readout is waiting before you open it. That keeps the "before you even ask" moment from the brief's 11:20 AM block as what comes next.

**Then a real company, live.** One beat in the core switches to a real YC company from the list Ken approves, paired with its synthetic room ([yc-rooms](yc-rooms.md); [writers' brief](../../features/20261022.01_hackathon_plan/00_brief_for_writers.md), 2:49 PM block). Its outside-world panel runs live on stage: the company's website, Hacker News, the App Store and SEC filings, read as the judges watch. Ken says out loud that the company is real and its data room is not: we wrote the room, every page says "Synthetic test document. Not this company's data.", and its problems are our test plants. Hollis Voice stays the controlled room whose planted flags are known. Why this form: judges remember what they saw working, and opening on a forward started by hand spends the first 20 seconds on the one thing on screen that is not real ([critique_demo.md](../../features/20261022.01_hackathon_plan/critique_demo.md), finding 2; [critique_judge.md](../../features/20261022.01_hackathon_plan/critique_judge.md), finding 6).

**The real forward.** An agent builds the forwarding address before Saturday ([inbox-forward](inbox-forward.md)), and the readout fills in as results land ([inbound-unasked](inbound-unasked.md)). So the close can forward a real pitch email on stage. Whether the close shows it live or says it in one line is the overview page's card on the demo opening.

**The other forms:** open on the forwarded email, started by hand and said so; or open on the data room upload. The overview page carries the card ("The demo opening").
