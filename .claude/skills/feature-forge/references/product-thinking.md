# Product thinking before the prototype

Before writing the short brief, work out whether this is useful work to pursue.
Read the product goals, relevant research, current capabilities, and existing
project board. Reuse settled context. Investigate questions the repository,
data, or an experiment can answer before asking the human.

Keep a focused source note with the reasoning that matters:

- **User job:** Who needs this, what triggers the need, what they want to
  accomplish, and what they do today. Write the job plainly and mark uncertainty.
- **Evidence and assumptions:** Distinguish observed facts from beliefs about
  user needs, behavior, and value. Identify which uncertainty could change the
  recommendation. Requirements and architecture choices belong in later docs.
- **Priority:** Compare with named alternatives on the board. Explain what this
  unlocks and what choosing it displaces. Avoid invented urgency or estimates.
- **Options:** Consider an existing capability, adoption, a smaller change,
  building, or doing nothing when those are credible choices. Do not manufacture
  alternatives just to fill a template.
- **Recommendation:** State the best next action, supporting evidence, unresolved
  questions, and what could change the decision.

Preserve the human's corrections and settled decisions. A first description of
the job is a hypothesis, not permanent truth. Link this note from the short brief
and include useful reasoning in the composed human document. Scale depth to the
uncertainty. A well-understood request may need only a paragraph.

This is product reasoning, not a compulsory interview, premise vote, or approval
gate. Continue to the short brief and prototype within the agreed scope. If the
evidence favors another action, explain that recommendation and pursue work
already authorized. Ask only for a material decision that remains with the human.
