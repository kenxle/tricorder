# The pitch itself: expert knowledge

Written by the Tricorder team from the [pitch deck guide](../docs/reports/20261022.03_document_guides/pitch-deck.md), the [use of funds plan guide](../docs/reports/20261022.03_document_guides/use-of-funds-plan.md), the [cap table guide](../docs/reports/20261022.03_document_guides/cap-table.md), the [prior financing guide](../docs/reports/20261022.03_document_guides/prior-financing.md), [FRAMEWORKS.md](../docs/ongoing/FRAMEWORKS.md) entries 1.4 (burn against the hiring plan), 1.10 (cap table reconciliation), 2.1 and 2.2 (comps), and the SEC's [Form D page](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/filing-form-d-notice). The public pages they quote are linked below. This is the team's reading of those sources.

This file gives the model an investor's lens on the round: what is being sold, at what price, whether the money is in, and what it buys. Scripts remain the source of computed figures. Do not invent a benchmark that is not cited here.

## What an expert checks first

- **The round's arithmetic.** Post-money equals pre-money plus the raise. The new money's ownership is the raise divided by the post-money. A post-money SAFE takes its amount divided by its cap (FRAMEWORKS 1.10). The deck, the cap table's pro forma, the model and any term sheet should give the same numbers.
- **Whether the money is committed.** A round the deck calls closed should have a signed instrument behind every dollar. The SEC defines the moment of commitment for Form D: "For this purpose, the date of first sale is the date on which the first investor is irrevocably contractually committed to invest." ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/filing-form-d-notice)) A signed SAFE or stock purchase agreement is that commitment. A verbal yes is not.
- **What the price is paid for.** Valuation as a multiple of ARR, using ARR rebuilt from the customer file, not the deck's figure.
- **What the money buys.** Haje Kamps: "Put differently: If I’m going to invest $2 million into your startup, I want to know what that money buys me, in terms of progress for your company." ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/11/the-ask))

## Rules

- **Split the round into signed and unsigned.** Add up the signed instruments in the room and compare the total with the amount the deck calls raised or closed. Report three numbers: signed, committed but unsigned, and the gap. Only the signed part counts as closed. CRV lists "a spreadsheet number with no stock purchase agreement behind it" among the causes of a conflicting percentage ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/what-is-a-data-room)).
- **Check the round's story against the public record.** Compare the deck's round with the Form D (amount sold and date of first sale), the company's announcement and press reports. A press report that the company was still raising after the date the deck says the round closed is a difference to show, with both dates quoted. An associate quoted by [Startups Magazine](https://startupsmagazine.co.uk/how-to-build-a-data-room-that-closes-the-deal) checks the public record before opening a data room.
- **Rebuild ARR before computing a multiple.** Compute the post-money divided by rebuilt recurring ARR, and the post-money divided by the deck's ARR. Show both. a16z names the gap this exposes: "Numbers that aren’t consistent with what’s in the deck. For example, your deck says $2M in ARR, but your model shows $1.5M." ([a16z](https://a16z.com/the-insiders-guide-to-data-rooms-what-to-know-before-you-raise/)) On a SAFE round, the post-money cap is the price.
- **Report the multiple. Leave the verdict to the partner.** FRAMEWORKS 1.10 says the cap table entry must not comment on whether the valuation or the dilution is good. Comps from entries 2.1 and 2.2 give context. A Form D shows that a round happened. It does not show that the company is a fair comparison.
- **Show the effective pre-money when the pool grows.** Venture Hacks: "Many entrepreneurs think their pre-money valuation determines their percentage ownership of the company." ([Venture Hacks](https://venturehacks.com/topics/option-pool)) A pool created before the new money lowers the effective pre-money.
- **Tie the use of funds to a milestone and to the hiring plan.** The lines should add to the raise, with a time frame and dated milestones. Kamps: "You are not raising money to get 18 months of runway. You are raising money to hit a certain set of milestones." ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/11/the-ask)) Show the runway rebuilt with every planned hire (FRAMEWORKS 1.4) beside the deck's runway.
- **Read the financing papers against the pitch.** Hustle Fund lists "Financing documents whose economics differ materially from the pitch." as a reason to pause ([Hustle Fund](https://hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-angel-investing-due-diligence-checklist-step-by-step)).

## Benchmarks, with their context

- **The round should reach the next fundable milestone.** YC: "Their goal should be to raise as much money as needed to get to their next “fundable” milestone, which will usually be 12 to 18 months later." ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/)) YC wrote this about seed companies that need a follow-on round.
- **Around 18 months of operations.** MaC Venture Capital, a seed fund: "Typically, companies raise enough capital to fund operations for around 18 months." ([Kauffman Fellows](https://www.kauffmanfellows.org/journal/venture-diligence-at-the-seed-stage)) NFX asks for at least 18 to 24 months of runway ([NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/the-non-obvious-guide-to-fundraising)). Read the rebuilt runway against these, not the deck's.
- **No buffer threshold.** Kamps advised a 30 to 70 percent safety buffer in 2022 and 20 to 40 percent in 2023 ([2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/11/the-ask), [2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/27/use-of-funds-are-not-percentages)). One writer gave two answers, so Tricorder sets no line.
- **No valuation multiple benchmark.** None of the sources here gives a typical multiple of ARR by stage. Show the multiple and the comps beside it, with no line.

## Stage expectations

- **Seed:** the round is often SAFEs, and the price is the post-money cap. The deck may be the only place a number appears, so a claim with no second source reads as "not enough in the room". The use of funds is usually one slide. Kamps: "Don’t list a range." ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/11/the-ask))
- **Series A:** a priced round with a term sheet and a pro forma cap table. AngelList: "This is a version of the cap table that shows the updated company ownership after a financing round closes." ([AngelList](https://learn.angellist.com/articles/cap-table)) CRV: "Every slide in your deck should pair vision with verifiable proof" ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/series-a-pitch-deck)). The ARR behind the multiple should rebuild from the customer file.

## What to ask

- The signed SAFE or stock purchase agreement for every dollar the deck calls raised, with dates.
- A list of commitments not yet signed: investor, amount and expected signing date.
- Bank statements that show the investor wires.
- The term sheet, with its option pool terms.
- The ARR build by customer behind the deck's ARR.
- How the money will be spent, by month and by category, with the milestones it reaches and the month the company expects to raise next.
- The Form D file number, or the exemption the company relied on.
