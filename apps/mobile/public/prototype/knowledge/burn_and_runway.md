# Burn and runway: expert knowledge

This file gives the model a qualitative investor lens for the company's spending and cash position. Scripts remain the source of computed figures. Do not invent numeric benchmarks where none are supplied.

## Historical financial statements

When opening the monthly P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow, start with how the company spends its money:

- Identify where the company spends the most.
- Understand the reason behind the largest spending areas.
- Look for deficiencies in the spending picture.

Treat these as questions to investigate in context, not automatic flags. No numeric threshold has been supplied for what a company should spend in any category.

The [historical financials guide](../docs/reports/20261022.03_document_guides/historical-financials.md) describes how to tie revenue, costs, cash movement, and financing together, and how expectations differ by stage.

## Stage lens

- **Seed:** assess how spending supports experiments that validate the business model.
- **Series A:** focus more on growth and what the spending is producing in growth.
- **Runway:** Kimmy supplied 18 months of runway after the round closes as a practical heuristic. This is a reasonable baseline, not a hard cutoff: check that the round funds the next milestone and leaves a buffer for fundraising, which can take many months. In a slower market or for a capital-intensive plan, consider a longer runway. a16z emphasizes sizing a round to milestones and giving the company cushion through a potentially lengthy fundraise ([fundraising questions](https://a16z.com/16-common-questions-about-fundraising/)); its downturn guidance recommends 24+ months ([efficient growth in a downturn](https://a16z.com/saas-businesses-weather-hard-times-by-focusing-on-efficient-growth/)). The 18-month figure itself is Kimmy's expert input, not an externally sourced universal threshold.
- Kimmy confirms using 18 months after close as the same baseline for seed and Series A, with longer runway when milestone or fundraising conditions call for it.

This stage distinction comes from expert input in this working session; no numeric burn or runway threshold has been supplied.

### Confirmed evidence for each stage

Kimmy confirmed this recommendation.

- **Seed experiments:** judge whether each experiment tests a specific assumption with the intended customer and produces observable learning or behavior. Willingness to try or pay is stronger evidence than positive feedback alone. YC describes the MVP as a learning step and says the goal is to test whether the product actually solves an important problem for a chosen customer ([How to get and test ideas](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7x-how-to-get-and-test-ideas)).
- **Series A growth:** look for evidence that the company can repeat its growth motion, not only that it has grown once. For B2B SaaS, Bessemer describes Series A/B as the stage to demonstrate a repeatable sales cycle, with metrics such as sales-cycle length and net revenue retention ([CFO Playbook](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/cfo-playbook-mastering-metrics-and-managing-boards-for-saas-finance-success)). Apply equivalent business-model-specific measures to games and other verticals; do not import SaaS benchmarks without a fit.

This is a sourced recommendation for what evidence to examine, not a universal numeric growth target.

## Expert lens: spending against milestones

Kimmy confirmed this lens.

Compare actual spending with the company's stated plan, current bottleneck, and next milestone. Ask what each major spend category is meant to accomplish, what progress it bought, and whether the company can still deliver its plan with the resources it has. A deficiency is a mismatch between the plan's critical work and the people or money assigned to it, not simply a low spend figure.

- **B2B:** connect sales and marketing spend to customer acquisition and conversion, and connect product, implementation, and customer-success spend to delivering value and converting target customers into paid customers. A large cost or an absent function needs an explanation in the context of the sales motion.
- **Gaming:** connect development and launch spend to the ability to ship a quality game and reach its audience. Compare launch or marketing spending with the product's readiness and the evidence of audience demand.
- **Across stages:** watch for headcount or other major costs rising without corresponding progress on the milestone they are supposed to support. Do not flag a category solely because it is high or low compared with a generic percentage.

This is a recommendation, not a fixed benchmark. a16z advises seed-stage companies to tie cash management to the milestones needed for the next raise and revisit the forecast as conditions change ([How Founders Should Think About Cash Management](https://a16z.com/how-founders-should-think-about-cash-management/)). YC highlights revenue, net burn, and runway as core metrics, while noting that some early consumer companies may prioritize active users before revenue ([Key Startup Metrics](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/KR-key-startup-metrics)). The metric that indicates progress depends on the company's model and stage.

## Expert lens: reconcile the statements

Kimmy confirmed this lens.

Look for one coherent path from operating results to cash on hand. A timing difference can be normal; an unexplained mismatch is a question for the founder, not an automatic flag.

- **Cash movement:** reconcile beginning cash plus operating, investing, and financing cash flows to ending cash, then tie ending cash to bank statements. If calculated burn does not explain the bank movement, ask which financing inflow, one-time payment, or timing difference is missing. [Hustle Fund on burn and runway](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-startup-burn-rate-management-what-investors-should-actually-watch-for).
- **Revenue and collections:** compare P&L revenue with billings, collections, accounts receivable, and deferred revenue. Cash received in advance is not automatically earned revenue; revenue recognition should follow delivery under the company's accounting basis. [FASB Technical Bulletin 90-1](https://storage.fasb.org/ftb%2090-1.pdf); [the historical financials guide](../docs/reports/20261022.03_document_guides/historical-financials.md).
- **Profit and operating cash:** understand why net income or loss differs from operating cash flow, including working-capital movement and non-cash items. Tie any difference to source schedules rather than accepting an unexplained plug.
- **Financing:** confirm that debt or equity proceeds on the cash flow statement match the financing history and the balance sheet's debt and equity balances.
- **Consistency:** compare periods on a consistent accounting basis, and ask why prior statements changed materially during fundraising.

a16z recommends monthly P&L history that traces gross revenue through loss and cash outflow, with revenue types and major costs broken out ([The Insider's Guide to Data Rooms](https://a16z.com/the-insiders-guide-to-data-rooms-what-to-know-before-you-raise/)). A clear reconciliation supports trust in the operating story; a gap without an explanation weakens it.

## Confirmed downside-case review

Kimmy confirmed this driver-based stress-test approach.

Stress the assumptions that could prevent the next milestone, rather than applying an arbitrary percentage haircut. For B2B, consider a longer sales cycle, lower conversion to paid customers, or delayed customer launches. For gaming, consider a delayed ship date, weaker sales or revenue than same-genre peers, or higher development costs. Carry each scenario through revenue, hiring, cash flow, runway, and milestone timing so the reader can see what decision changes.

This approach follows the model guide's requirement for a downside case. Hustle Fund recommends changing a small set of meaningful drivers and carrying them through all three statements, with monthly visibility through the next milestone and financing decision ([Startup Financial Projections](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-financial-projections-for-angel-investors-models-that-build-confidence)). a16z likewise ties cash planning to milestones and monthly reforecasting ([Cash Management](https://a16z.com/how-founders-should-think-about-cash-management/)).
