# Team: expert knowledge

Written by the Tricorder team from the [founder bios guide](../docs/reports/20261022.03_document_guides/founder-bios.md), the [key team bios and org chart guide](../docs/reports/20261022.03_document_guides/key-team-bios-and-org-chart.md), the [hiring plan and future org guide](../docs/reports/20261022.03_document_guides/hiring-plan-and-future-org.md), the [employment, advisory and option grant records guide](../docs/reports/20261022.03_document_guides/employment-advisory-and-option-grant-records.md), and [FRAMEWORKS.md](../docs/ongoing/FRAMEWORKS.md) entries 1.4 (burn against the hiring plan), 6.1 (founder-market fit) and 6.2 (passion and commitment). The public pages they quote are linked below. This is the team's reading of those sources.

This file gives the model an investor's lens on the people who will spend the money. Scripts remain the source of computed figures. Do not invent a benchmark that is not cited here.

## What an expert checks first

- **Why these founders, for this problem.** Hustle Fund: "The best founders often have personal experience with the pain point." ([Hustle Fund](https://www2.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-how-hustle-fund-reviews-1000-startups-per-month-for-its-angel-investing-group)) FRAMEWORKS 6.1 builds the answer from dated public work: roles, repos, talks and writing that predate the company.
- **Whether the story matches the public record.** An associate quoted by Startups Magazine: "Before I even open your data room, I already have ten tabs up. I’m checking your traffic, reviews, investors, any co-founder issues, and whether your story matches what’s online." ([Startups Magazine](https://startupsmagazine.co.uk/how-to-build-a-data-room-that-closes-the-deal)) That includes team size. The headcount in the deck should agree with the model, the org chart and the company's own public posts.
- **Who owns each function.** Hustle Fund: "Role coverage: Who owns product, engineering, sales, operations, and finance? Which missing role becomes urgent after the raise?" ([Hustle Fund checklist](https://hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-angel-investing-due-diligence-checklist-step-by-step))
- **Whether the model pays for the hiring plan.** Kruze: "Headcount is often 60-80% of total burn. So if your forecast doesn’t model hiring clearly, it’s basically a guess." ([Kruze](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/build-a-rolling-cash-forecast/))

## Rules

- **Count people the same way in every file.** Compare staff today across the deck, the model, the org chart and the public record, such as a launch post or a team page. A number two independent sources state the same way is corroborated ([FRAMEWORKS.md](../docs/ongoing/FRAMEWORKS.md), "Piece verdicts"). The deck, the overview, the video and the website repeat one another, so together they count as one source.
- **Rebuild payroll from the hiring plan.** FRAMEWORKS 1.4 costs every planned hire from its start month, reruns the cash, and compares the runway with the model and the deck. Report how many planned hires the model's burn carries, and the runway with all of them paid. Startups.com names the failure: "Hiring plan disconnected from financials : company has a hiring plan but doesn't tie it to burn-rate or runway models. Plan and financial model diverge." ([Launchrock, vendor view](https://www.launchrock.com/library/expert-advice/hiring-plan)) A hire left out of the model is a difference between two files. Show both runways.
- **Ask what each hire is for.** Hustle Fund: "“Hire and grow” is not a financing plan." ([Hustle Fund](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-red-flags-in-startup-founders-that-experienced-investors-never-ignore)) Visible: "Seed and Series A founders also need bios for the key team members, an org chart, and a hiring plan tied directly to the use of funds." ([Visible, vendor view](https://visible.vc/blog/what-to-include-in-your-data-room/))
- **Read key-person risk from the papers.** Check who is full time. Cooley's request list: "A list of officers and directors. If any officers are not currently devoting 100% of their business time to the Company, please note them on this list." ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/documents/sample-vc-due-diligence-request-list/)). Check founder vesting. NASPP: "A founder who owns all their equity outright could, in theory, walk away tomorrow without consequence." ([NASPP](https://www.naspp.com/blog/what-do-investors-look-for-on-your-cap-table)) Check that key people hold real equity and have retention terms.
- **Show where the skills sit.** A team weighted to one function is a fact to report, with any function that has no owner. Ask which role the raise hires first. Do not score the mix as good or bad.
- **Treat a mismatch as a question.** Hustle Fund: "treat a discrepancy as a prompt for context rather than an automatic finding of misconduct" ([Hustle Fund checklist](https://hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-angel-investing-due-diligence-checklist-step-by-step)). A thin public profile or missing GitHub activity is not a negative. Do not infer age, gender, ethnicity, nationality, religion, politics, health or family (FRAMEWORKS 6.1).

## Benchmarks, with their context

- **Load factor of 1.25 to 1.4.** Kruze: "a rough rule of thumb for many U.S. companies is 1.25-1.4 times base salary for fully loaded cost, but your actual multiplier will depend on your benefits and locations." ([Kruze](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/build-a-rolling-cash-forecast/)) One accounting firm's rule of thumb for U.S. companies.
- **Hires start late.** Kruze: "Build some slack into your model and assume most future hires start a month or two later than you would like." Tricorder uses this for a start-date slip scenario, not as a pass or fail.
- **Hiring pace is context only.** Point Nine's guest study of 15 European software companies found the average went from 4 people to 25 in 18 months. The author adds: "please don’t think that in order to be successful you must have 25 people on your team 18 months after founding." ([Point Nine, guest post](https://writing.pointnine.com/p/off-to-a-flying-start-who-should)) The sample picked winners.
- **No cutoff for team size, engineering share or C-level share.** No source in this set gives one. Show the ratio and set no threshold.

## Stage expectations

- **Seed:** the team carries the first pass. Hustle Fund: "At the earliest stages, the team carries unusual weight because the product and market approach may change." ([Hustle Fund checklist](https://hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-angel-investing-due-diligence-checklist-step-by-step)) An org chart is lighter. The hiring plan is short and tied to what the round must prove.
- **Series A:** the company's record speaks for the founders. The question becomes whether the model pays for every hire in the plan. Visible: "By Series A, employment and advisory agreements with IP assignment and key-person retention terms become standard diligence asks." Background checks and formal reference calls usually come after the term sheet ([SaaStr, Lemkin](https://www.saastr.com/how-when-why-vcs-do-due-diligence-pre-and-post-term-sheet)).

## What to ask

- A one-page bio for each founder with dated roles, and the public links they want read.
- A current org chart or payroll list that names everyone counted as staff today, with contractors marked.
- The hiring plan: role, department, start date, base pay and equity grant for each hire, and which rows the model pays for.
- What the founders would cut first if progress is slower ([Hustle Fund](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-red-flags-in-startup-founders-that-experienced-investors-never-ignore)).
- Founder vesting schedules, and retention terms for key people.
- Reference names, including one outside the founders' own circle. Jonathan Hua: "I always ask for at least one reference outside the people you’d naturally choose." ([The Data Room](https://thedataroom.substack.com/p/how-a-vc-does-due-diligence-on-your-company))
