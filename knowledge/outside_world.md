The public pages they quote are linked below. This is the team's reading of those sources, not an investor's own input.

This file gives the model an investor's lens on checking the room's figures against the public record. Scripts remain the source of computed figures. Do not invent a benchmark that is not cited here.

## What an expert checks first

Associates look outside before they look inside. One associate: "Before I even open your data room, I already have ten tabs up. I’m checking your traffic, reviews, investors, any co-founder issues, and whether your story matches what’s online." ([Startups Magazine](https://startupsmagazine.co.uk/how-to-build-a-data-room-that-closes-the-deal)) Before comparing a public fact with the room, establish:

- **Which public sources exist.** The pricing page, the YC company page and launch posts, Hacker News, SEC Form D filings, press, job boards and careers pages, and app stores.
- **Who wrote each one.** The company's website, blog, press releases, launch posts and founders' posts are the company's own words. They count with the deck as one source (outside-in slice; FRAMEWORKS.md "Piece verdicts"). A YC page's batch listing is YC's record. Its description is the company's.
- **When each was written.** A launch post or press story can be months older than the room.
- **Whether the two figures measure the same thing.** Same metric, same period, same unit.

## Rules

- **Agreement is a small green flag.** A room figure that matches a source the company did not write is corroborated: a Form D amount, an outlet's own reporting, a customer's own page, an app store record (outside-in slice). It shows the figure was reported the same way twice. It does not prove the figure is true, because a consistent fake passes (FRAMEWORKS.md 1.11). The company's own pages agreeing with the deck add nothing.
- **Disagreement is a big red flag.** In "Piece verdicts" contradiction wins: two of the company's own files agreeing does not cancel a check that found otherwise. Consilium Law, on a contract that differs from a deck: "Where they disagree, the disagreement is the finding." ([Consilium Law](https://consilium.law/sparkpoint/startup-fundraising-legal-due-diligence-checklist/)) CRV on outside calls: "One customer reference that contradicts a retention claim can undo confidence late in diligence." ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/what-is-a-data-room))
- **Name both dates before calling a difference.** Compare an older public figure with a newer room figure only with both dates shown (outside-in slice). Growth since a launch post is not a contradiction.
- **Check published prices against the room.** FRAMEWORKS.md 3.2 asks whether the pricing page matches the plans in the room's own files. Compare the page with the prices in contracts and the customer file too. A plan that says "contact sales" has no price to compare.
- **Check the round against Form D.** Compare the deck's amount raised with the Form D amount sold. If the company raised under Regulation D, a Form D is due 15 days after the first sale (BCLP, in the [prior financing guide](../docs/reports/20261022.03_document_guides/prior-financing.md)). BCLP: "an issuer that fails to file a Form D will almost certainly have failed to make a proper blue sky notice filing". ([BCLP](https://bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/sec-enforcement-actions-for-failure-to-file-form-d-a-wake-up-call-for-startups-and-private-fund-advisers.html))
- **A missing record is a gap, not a flag.** No Form D found may mean the filing is not due yet, sits under a different legal name, or the round used another exemption. The outside-in slice says no Form D is never a flag on its own. It becomes a question for the founder and, if needed, a lawyer.
- **Round status is a fact to compare.** Press that says the company is still raising, against a room that says the round closed, is a disagreement once both dates are named. Ask for the closing papers.
- **Check job posts against the hiring plan.** FRAMEWORKS.md 3.1 counts open roles by function. Roles posted that the plan lacks, or a plan with hires and no posts, are questions. One reading gives no direction.
- **An empty footprint is a finding, not a weakness.** A blocked or empty source is recorded as no signal, never as zero (outside-in slice; FRAMEWORKS.md 3.1).
- **Report differences, never motives.** Tricorder finds differences in reporting. It does not call anything fraud (pitch deck guide).

## Benchmarks, with their context

No source gives a numeric line for how closely public figures should match. These are the fixed points:

- **Form D timing.** 15 calendar days after the first sale, per BCLP, a law firm (prior financing guide). The checklist item is `form_d_lookup`.
- **Form D accuracy.** The SEC says it cannot guarantee the accuracy of its Form D data sets (outside-in slice, citing [Form D data sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-d-data-sets), paraphrased). A matching Form D is strong corroboration, not proof.
- **How much a mismatch costs.** The same associate: "If revenue in the deck doesn’t match the P&L, or you show only part of your history, everything else becomes questionable." ([Startups Magazine](https://startupsmagazine.co.uk/how-to-build-a-data-room-that-closes-the-deal)) She is talking about the room. The team applies the same weight to a public record that contradicts it.
- **Tolerance.** Two figures agree when they sit within `match_tolerance`, a setting (FRAMEWORKS.md "Piece verdicts").

## Stage expectations

- **Seed:** the public record is thin. A website, a YC page, a launch post and a funding announcement may be all there is. Each one the company wrote counts as its own words. A seed round may have no Form D yet, or one under a legal name the deck does not use.
- **Series A:** more to check. Earlier rounds, press, competitors and job posts usually exist. The prior financing guide asks for evidence each earlier raise was filed correctly, such as a Form D and state notices, or the exemption relied on.

## What to ask

- The legal entity name and state used for securities filings.
- Form D filing dates for each round, or the exemption relied on instead.
- The closing documents for the latest round, when press and the room disagree on its status.
- The date and source behind any public figure that differs from the room.
- The current price list, beside what customers pay under their contracts.
